# Wave 9 — comparative court-system matrix

Rows are systems. Columns are structural questions, not model names. Values are `YES`, `NO`,
`PARTIAL`, `SEPARATE`, `MERGED` and `NOT ESTABLISHED`.

`NOT ESTABLISHED` is never rendered as `NO`. Several dossiers say explicitly that appeal routes
and detailed competences were not researched from primary sources; those cells stay open.

## A. How many hierarchies, and what decides which one hears a case

| System            | One pyramid?       | What decides the route                                     | Apex arrangement                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ----------------- | ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Germany**       | **NO**             | The branch of jurisdiction                                 | Art. 92 GG vests judicial power in three distinct categories — the Federal Constitutional Court, the federal courts, and the courts of the Länder. Art. 95(1) names **five** federal supreme courts, each heading a separate branch |
| **France**        | **NO**             | The nature of the dispute, not its location or seriousness | Two court orders with separate hierarchies; the Cour de cassation heads the judicial order                                                                                                                                          |
| **Netherlands**   | **NO**             | Whether the matter is administrative                       | Hoge Raad for civil, criminal and tax; administrative law does **not** funnel into it — separate highest administrative courts exist                                                                                                |
| **Brazil**        | **NO**             | Federal/state, plus three subject-matter branches          | STF at the apex; STJ for non-constitutional federal law; labour, electoral and military justice each with their own hierarchy                                                                                                       |
| **United States** | **NO**             | Chiefly the law at issue                                   | Two court systems — federal and each state's                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Spain**         | **Closest to yes** | Ordinary vs constitutional                                 | Art. 117.5 makes _unidad jurisdiccional_ the basis of organisation: one national judiciary, no separate Autonomous Community court systems                                                                                          |
| **Japan**         | PARTIAL            | Tier and case type                                         | Supreme Court at the apex; family and summary courts are distinct parts, not divisions of the ordinary courts                                                                                                                       |

## B. Constitutional review — four arrangements, not one

| System          | Separate constitutional court? | Who may review legislation                                                                                                                                               | Mode                                                                                                            |
| --------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Germany**     | **SEPARATE**                   | Bundesverfassungsgericht — named separately in Art. 92 and **not** among Art. 95(1)'s five supreme courts                                                                | Constitutional jurisdiction                                                                                     |
| **Spain**       | **SEPARATE**                   | Tribunal Constitucional, **outside** the ordinary judiciary and expressly "not the top of the court system"                                                              | Constitutionality of laws and constitutional rights                                                             |
| **Belgium**     | **SEPARATE**                   | Constitutional Court of twelve judges; may **annul and suspend** legislation                                                                                             | Conformity of acts, decrees and ordinances; division of powers                                                  |
| **Brazil**      | **MERGED**                     | STF is both the highest court and the constitutional court                                                                                                               | **Both** abstract (ação direta de inconstitucionalidade, ação declaratória de constitucionalidade) and concrete |
| **Japan**       | **MERGED**                     | Supreme Court holds constitutional review — but exercises it **only when deciding an actual case**, not in the abstract. No separate constitutional court                | Concrete only                                                                                                   |
| **Ireland**     | **DISPERSED**                  | The **High Court** has jurisdiction to determine the validity of any law having regard to the Constitution                                                               | Concrete, at first instance                                                                                     |
| **Netherlands** | **NONE, BY DESIGN**            | **Article 120 forbids the courts from reviewing the constitutionality of Acts of Parliament.** The place a constitutional court occupies elsewhere is deliberately empty | None over Acts of Parliament                                                                                    |

## C. Federal and sub-national arrangements — five different designs

| System            | Design                             | The distinguishing feature                                                                                                                                                               |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **United States** | Two parallel systems               | Federal and state judiciaries each complete; most litigation is in state courts because most law is state law. The federal three-tier shape is **not** a template the states must follow |
| **Australia**     | Investiture, not duplication       | Rather than build a full parallel system, the Commonwealth invests **state** courts with federal jurisdiction under s.77(iii)                                                            |
| **Switzerland**   | Cantonal institutions, federal law | The cantons organise and run the courts (Arts. 122–123); since 1 January 2011 a unified federal Criminal Procedure Code applies across all of them                                       |
| **Germany**       | Land-administered, federal law     | Most cases are heard in Land courts administered by the Länder, applying federal statutes. **Court administration and adjudicative hierarchy are separate things**                       |
| **Brazil**        | Both, plus specialised             | Federal justice (Art. 106) and state justice (Art. 125) run together, with three specialised branches alongside                                                                          |
| **Spain**         | Explicitly none                    | Art. 117.5 _unidad jurisdiccional_. The counter-case: a decentralised state whose judiciary is not decentralised                                                                         |

| System                        | Separate administrative courts? | Apex                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Germany                       | YES                             | Federal Administrative Court, one of the five under Art. 95(1)                                                                                                |
| France                        | YES                             | A distinct court order; which order hears a case depends on the nature of the dispute                                                                         |
| Netherlands                   | YES                             | **Three** separate highest administrative courts, including the Administrative Jurisdiction Division of the Council of State and the Central Appeals Tribunal |

## E. Subject-matter specialisation

| System      | Specialised branches                                                                                                                                           |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Germany** | Labour, social and fiscal jurisdictions each have their own federal supreme court under Art. 95(1); Art. 96 permits a Federal Patent Court and military courts |
| **Brazil**  | Labour (TST), electoral (TSE) and military (STM) justice, each a full hierarchy                                                                                |
| **Japan**   | Family courts (domestic relations and juvenile) and summary courts, distinct parts of the system rather than divisions of the ordinary courts                  |
| **Spain**   | The Audiencia Nacional, a specialised national court within the single judiciary                                                                               |

## F. Court administration is not adjudication

| System  | Who administers                                                                                                                                                                           | Who adjudicates                                                   |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Germany | The Länder administer the courts that apply federal law                                                                                                                                   | Adjudicative hierarchy runs by branch, not by administrator       |
| Spain   | The **CGPJ** is the governing body — appointments, discipline, administration. Art. 122 makes this explicit, and the dossier records that it **is not a court and does not decide cases** | The Tribunal Supremo heads the ordinary judiciary                 |
| Canada  | Provincial and federal responsibilities differ                                                                                                                                            | Supreme Court Act s.4 and s.6 govern the apex court's composition |

## G. What the matrix forces

**1. There is no single court pyramid.** Six of the twelve systems here have more than one
hierarchy, and what decides the route is usually the _nature of the dispute_ rather than its
seriousness or location. A diagram of one hierarchy describes Germany's courts about as well as a
photograph of one wheel describes a car.

**2. Constitutional court and supreme court are different institutions in some systems and the
same one in others.** Germany, Spain and Belgium keep them separate; Brazil and Japan merge them;
Ireland disperses the function to the High Court; the Netherlands forbids it outright. Calling the
BVerfG "the German supreme court" is wrong twice — it is not among the five, and the five are not
one court.

**3. "Supreme court" does not name one function.** The Cour de cassation reviews whether the law
was correctly applied rather than re-deciding cases; the STF is an apex court _and_ a
constitutional court; the Swiss Federal Supreme Court exists to ensure the uniform application of
federal law across cantonal institutions. The label travels better than the function.

**4. Federal systems do not solve the same problem the same way.** The United States duplicates,
Australia invests, Switzerland unifies the law and leaves the institutions cantonal, Germany
separates administration from hierarchy, and Spain declines to decentralise at all.

**5. Several systems say plainly what has not been established.** Canada's appeal routes and
appointment process, Switzerland's cantonal detail, Spain's higher-court composition and
Germany's Land-tier competences are all recorded in the corpus as unresearched. Those gaps are
carried forward as gaps.
