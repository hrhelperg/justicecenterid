# Singapore — model findings

What Singapore added to the comparative picture, and what it reused.

## 1. A new prosecution shape: adviser and prosecutor fused in one officer

Batch B mapped the civil-law prosecution spectrum; New Zealand added the common-law Law-Officer
model. Singapore adds a further variant: the **Attorney-General is at once the Government's legal
adviser (Art. 35(7)) and the Public Prosecutor (Art. 35(8); Criminal Procedure Code s 11)**. One
constitutional officer advises the executive and controls all criminal prosecutions — a deliberate
fusion no other country on the site currently shows. The office is insulated (appointed from persons
qualified as a Supreme Court judge, removable only via a judicial tribunal, not a Cabinet minister),
which is how the system reconciles the fusion with prosecutorial independence. Carried by
`prosecutionScope: 'own'` plus prose.

## 2. The clearest use of the site's "record the absence" rule

Singapore's oversight page is defined as much by what is missing as by what exists: **no general
public-sector ombudsman, no Paris-Principles national human-rights institution, and no dedicated
independent police-complaints commission**. The site's presence-state model exists precisely for
this — "absent, recorded explicitly, because a blank would be indistinguishable from unresearched" —
and Singapore is where that rule does the most work. The page states the three absences as researched
negatives and adds the institutions that DO exist (the CPIB in the Prime Minister's Office; the
Presidential Council for Minority Rights). It also **excludes** the contested 2026 UPR debate over
whether Singapore should create an NHRI, because the Government's formal response could not be
independently confirmed — honesty by omission where a source was unverifiable.

## 3. A third common-law police-led investigation

Like New Zealand, Singapore separates investigating from charging: the police (and specialist
agencies) investigate, and the Public Prosecutor evaluates the evidence and decides. This reinforces
the "who directs a criminal investigation" comparison — common-law police-led versus civil-law
prosecutor/magistrate-led — now with two clean common-law examples in one batch.

## 4. Diffuse review, no constitutional court, parliamentary-influenced apex

Singapore reuses the diffuse-review / no-constitutional-court shape (the ordinary Supreme Court
decides constitutional questions), with the Court of Appeal as the final court and the High Court
split into Appellate and General Divisions after the 2019/2021 restructuring. No schema change.

## 5. World Prison Brief, second stale-capacity case

Singapore's capacity figure is from 2013 while the population is 2024, so — like New Zealand earlier
in the batch — the restricted claim states population + rate and makes no current occupancy claim,
explicitly declining to repeat the source's stale 79.2%. Two Batch C countries (NZ, SG) now
demonstrate the stale-capacity handling, and one (South Korea) the valid same-date occupancy — the
same source, three honest treatments.

## Net schema change

**None.** Singapore reused the unitary country-level record; the adviser/prosecutor fusion, the
recorded absences, and the diffuse review all live in prose and in the tests.
