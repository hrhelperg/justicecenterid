# Austria — model findings

What Austria added to the comparative picture, and what it reused.

## 1. The second "federal but justice-federal" state — reuse of the Belgium model

Austria is a federation of nine Länder, but the four justice functions are federal competences of
both legislation and execution (B-VG Art. 10(1) Z 6+Z 7, Art. 82(1)). This is the **Belgium**
pattern, and the explicit **inverse of Germany**:

|                                | Germany                                           | Austria / Belgium                           |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| State form                     | federal                                           | federal                                     |
| Who runs courts/police/prisons | the Länder (Germany's federal record is `shared`) | the Federation (`own`, `exclusive-federal`) |
| Sub-national justice records   | yes (Land records)                                | **none**                                    |

So Austria is modelled exactly as Belgium: a single `federal` record, every function `own`, every
core `legislativeCompetence` `exclusive-federal`, and **no** Land record. This confirms that the
Belgium model generalises — the "federal vs. justice-centralised" distinction is carried entirely by
scope values, needing no new schema. The one devolved element (each Land organises its own
administrative court, Art. 129) is a court-organisation carve-out noted in prose, not a Land record —
a record exists only where it does the core institutional work.

## 2. Three co-equal apex courts — a shape the model already tolerates

Austria has **three** courts of last resort, none superior to the others: OGH (civil/criminal),
VfGH (constitutional), VwGH (administrative). The dossier carries this in prose using the OGH's own
words ("hierarchically on the same level"). No schema change: the jurisdiction model never asserted
a single supreme court, so parallel apex courts fit without modification. Italy (three top courts)
and Portugal (four) already stress-tested this; Austria is a clean reuse.

## 3. A prosecution under the executive — the third distinct answer in Batch B

Batch B now holds three different answers to "where does the prosecution sit?":

- **Italy** — inside the independent judiciary (magistrate-prosecutors).
- **Portugal** — constitutionally autonomous (neither judiciary nor executive).
- **Austria** — an organ of the ordinary judiciary but **subordinate to the Federal Minister of
  Justice** through a statutory chain of instruction (StAG §2(1)).

Austria is the clearest "under the executive" case, and the dossier states it plainly rather than
softening it. It also carries, neutrally and by attribution, a **2026 consultation draft** (not
enacted) to make the service independent — the same enacted-but-conditional / proposed-not-yet-law
discipline used for Italy's Nordio reform: described in prose, never as a scheduled change, because
its commencement is not fixed.

## 4. Honest prison numbers when the source is incomplete

Austria is the one Batch B country for which SPACE I reported **no capacity**. Rather than skip the
figure or manufacture a density, the restricted claim states the population (9,258) and the rate
(101.1/100k) and explicitly makes **no** occupancy or density claim. A test asserts that the
statement never says "over/under capacity". This is the corrections-page equivalent of an honest
deferral: report exactly what the source supports, and say where it stops.

## Net schema change

**None.** Austria reused the Belgium `federal`/`exclusive-federal` model, the multi-apex-court prose
pattern, and the pending-reform prose discipline.
