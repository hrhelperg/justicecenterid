# France — police oversight, internal inspection and external rights review

**Status:** research complete, 2026-08-10. Wave 6.

This document was written from French primary instruments read in French. The English labels
used elsewhere on this platform — _internal affairs_, _inspectorate_, _oversight body_,
_independent investigation_ — were deliberately **not** used as the starting frame. They are
tested against the evidence in §7 and in
`docs/research/wave-6-oversight-taxonomy-findings.md`, and most of them fail.

## 2. IGPN — Inspection générale de la Police nationale

### Position

Décret n° 2013-784, art. 1 (as modified by décret n° 2023-530 of 29 June 2023, in force
1 July 2023):

> « L'inspection générale de la police nationale est un service actif de la direction générale
> de la police nationale. »

This is the single most important sentence about the IGPN, and it is the one that English
labels destroy. The IGPN is **a service of the police force it examines**. It is headed by a
_directeur des services actifs de la police nationale_. Its scope covers the services of the
DGPN, the préfecture de police, and — under conditions set by article 5 — the DGSI.

### Functions

- **Inspection, study, audit, advice** (art. 3): « une mission générale d'inspection, d'étude,
  d'audit et de conseil ». It also steers internal control and risk management by delegation
  from the DGPN and the préfet de police.
- **Judicial (criminal) investigation** (art. 4): « L'inspection générale de la police
  nationale diligente des enquêtes judiciaires, d'initiative ou sur instruction de l'autorité
  judiciaire. » Note _d'initiative_ — it may open a criminal investigation itself.
- **Complaints and denunciations** (art. 4): « Elle reçoit les plaintes et dénonciations. »
- **Administrative investigation** (art. 5, as modified by décret n° 2025-651 of 16 July 2025):
  conducted **on instruction** of the Minister of the Interior, the DGPN, the DGSI or the
  préfet de police, with a narrow urgency exception allowing prefects and territorial service
  heads to contact the head of the IGPN directly.

### Structure

The consolidated arrêté of 28 August 2013 places the IGPN under a _directeur des services
actifs_ and organises it in three sub-directorates, one of which — _enquêtes administratives
et judiciaires_ — contains a coordination unit, a **délégation nationale anti-corruption** and
**eight investigation delegations** (Bordeaux, Lille, Lyon, Marseille, Metz, Paris, Rennes,
Fort-de-France, with an office at Nice).

The anti-corruption delegation carries that name only since the arrêté of 26 August 2025,
in force 1 September 2025, which renamed the former _division nationale des enquêtes_. Some
official prose pages still use the old name. **Where a ministry web page and a consolidated
legal text disagree, this platform follows the consolidated legal text and says so.**

### The reporting platform is not a complaints mechanism

The IGPN operates an online _plateforme de signalement_, open to « Toute personne, victime ou
témoin — direct ou indirect —, quel que soit son lieu de résidence ». The ministry's own
description of it is explicit about what it is not:

> « Il ne s'agit ni d'un service d'urgence, ni d'un service d'enquête, ni d'un service de
> plainte. »

Its agents' role is to route the report « vers la hiérarchie compétente, pour y donner suite,
ou vers une délégation de l'IGPN ».

This matters more than it looks. The décret says the IGPN _reçoit les plaintes et
dénonciations_; the platform says it is _not_ a complaint service. Both are true of different
channels — a _plainte_ in the French sense is a criminal complaint, and the platform is an
administrative reporting route that expressly does not constitute one. A page that rendered
either sentence as "the IGPN takes complaints from the public" would flatten a distinction the
French state takes care to draw.

## 3. IGGN — Inspection générale de la Gendarmerie nationale

### Position

Code de la défense, art. D3122-12:

> « Le directeur général de la gendarmerie nationale dispose de l'inspection générale de la
> gendarmerie nationale. »

Again internal: the IGGN belongs to the director general of the force it examines, and it also
carries out the instructions of the Minister of the Interior. Article D3122-14 records that it
may receive investigation requests from the judicial authority concerning offences by
gendarmerie personnel, in or outside service.

Its attributions are set by the **arrêté of 23 avril 2025, which abrogated the arrêté of
15 janvier 2019**. Any description resting on the 2019 text is out of date.

### Functions

- **Inspection, audit, study, investigation, evaluation, expertise**, across employment of
  resources, human resources, administration, logistics and finance, security of installations
  and information systems, protection of personal data, personnel health and safety, and
  preservation of the environment. This is a very wide organisational remit, and it is not a
  misconduct remit.
- **Deontology**: it is « chargée du respect des règles de déontologie », conducts studies and
  makes the recommendations those rules require.
- **Administrative investigation**: « diligente, sur instruction ou d'initiative, les enquêtes
  administratives se rapportant au non-respect de ces règles ».
- **Referral to the judicial authority**: it « informe l'autorité judiciaire lorsque les
  manquements constatés sont susceptibles de constituer une infraction pénale ».
- **Judicial investigation**: « elle diligente les enquêtes judiciaires qui lui sont confiées
  et satisfait aux réquisitions judiciaires » — those _entrusted to it_.
- **Complaints from the public**: « assure le recueil et le traitement des doléances que lui
  adressent les particuliers », and collects reports from gendarmerie personnel who are
  victims or witnesses of harassment, discrimination or violence within the institution.
- An _observatoire de la gendarmerie pour l'égalité et contre les discriminations_ is attached
  to it organically (art. 3).

### The asymmetry with the IGPN, which is real and runs both ways

|                              | IGPN                                                | IGGN                                                      |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Criminal investigation       | may act **d'initiative** or on judicial instruction | only those **confiées** to it, plus judicial requisitions |
| Administrative investigation | **on instruction** (urgency exception aside)        | **on instruction or d'initiative**                        |
| Express deontology mandate   | not established in the instruments read             | express                                                   |

Two national police forces in one country, with inspectorates whose initiative runs in
opposite directions depending on whether the matter is criminal or administrative. This is the
detail that disappears the moment both are called "internal affairs".

## 4. Défenseur des droits

### Position

Constitution, art. 71-1, first paragraph:

> « Le Défenseur des droits veille au respect des droits et libertés par les administrations de
> l'État, les collectivités territoriales, les établissements publics, ainsi que par tout
> organisme investi d'une mission de service public, ou à l'égard duquel la loi organique lui
> attribue des compétences. »

Appointed by the President of the Republic for a **six-year, non-renewable** mandate,
incompatible with membership of the government or parliament; he may be seized by any person
who believes themselves harmed, and may act _d'office_; he reports on his activity to the
President of the Republic and to Parliament.

### The status wording, precisely

The current consolidated text of article 2 of loi organique n° 2011-333 reads:

> « Le Défenseur des droits, autorité administrative indépendante, ne reçoit et ne sollicite,
> dans l'exercice de ses attributions, aucune instruction. »

**"Autorité administrative indépendante", not "autorité constitutionnelle indépendante"** — in
the text as it now stands. The office itself is created by the Constitution; its statutory
classification is that of an independent administrative authority. Both halves have to be
stated, and neither on its own is accurate. He and his deputies « ne peuvent être poursuivis,
recherchés, arrêtés, détenus ou jugés à l'occasion des opinions qu'ils émettent ou des actes
qu'ils accomplissent dans l'exercice de leurs fonctions ».

### Competence, which is general and not police-specific

Article 4 gives five mandates. Police oversight is **one part of one of them** — 4°:

> « De veiller au respect de la déontologie par les personnes exerçant des activités de
> sécurité sur le territoire de la République »

And "personnes exerçant des activités de sécurité" is broader than the police. The
institution's own statement of competence lists: « Les policiers nationaux et municipaux, Les
gendarmes, Les agents de l'administration pénitentiaire, Les douaniers, Les agents de
surveillance des transports en commun, Les membres de services d'ordre, Les employés de
services de sécurité privée ».

**Private security employees and prison staff are inside this mandate.** A body whose security
remit covers private guards is not a police oversight body, and calling it one would be a
category error, not a simplification.

Within security deontology the Défenseur is assisted by a **collège**, which he chairs.

### Powers

Ascending, and each distinct:

- request explanations from any person (art. 18); require communication of useful information
  and documents (art. 20);
- conduct **vérifications sur place** in the administrative or private premises of the persons
  concerned (art. 22);
- assess whether the facts call for intervention (art. 24);
- make **recommendations**; and where a recommendation is not acted on, **enjoin** the person
  concerned to take the necessary measures within a set period; where the injunction is not
  followed, draw up a **rapport spécial** and make it public (art. 25);
- **refer the matter to the authority vested with disciplinary power** (art. 29);
- where the facts appear to constitute a crime or délit, **inform the procureur de la
  République**;
- present written or oral observations before civil, administrative and criminal courts, on
  their invitation or at the parties' request;
- report annually to the President of the Republic and the presidents of both chambers
  (art. 36).

What is absent is as important: **no power to impose a disciplinary sanction.** Article 29
locates that power explicitly in another authority. The Défenseur recommends, enjoins,
publishes, refers — and that is the ceiling.

## 5. CGLPL — Contrôleur général des lieux de privation de liberté

Created by loi n° 2007-1545 du 30 octobre 2007, an **autorité indépendante** charged with
verifying the conditions in which persons deprived of liberty are held and transferred, and
ensuring respect for their fundamental rights. He receives instructions from no authority,
cannot be removed during his mandate, and cannot be prosecuted for opinions or acts in the
exercise of his functions.

He may visit **at any time** any place where persons are deprived of their liberty by decision
of a public authority; the statute uses that general formula rather than a list, and the
institution's own account of its remit includes **police, gendarmerie and customs custody
premises** alongside prisons, psychiatric establishments, detention centres for foreign
nationals, airport and port waiting zones, court holding cells and transfer vehicles.
Authorities may oppose a visit only for grave reasons which they must justify, and by
proposing a postponement.

Any natural person may bring facts to his attention, as may the Prime Minister, members of the
government, parliamentarians and several other authorities; he may also act on his own
initiative. He issues avis and recommendations, may propose changes to legislation and
regulation, and publishes an annual activity report.

**He has no power to sanction.** This is a monitoring and reporting body, and it is the only
external body in this document with a standing right of physical access to police custody.

## 6. Conseil supérieur de la magistrature — named for boundary, not covered in depth

Constitution art. 65 establishes the CSM with a formation for _magistrats du siège_ and one
for _magistrats du parquet_. The siège formation « statue comme conseil de discipline des
magistrats du siège »; the parquet formation « donne son avis sur les sanctions disciplinaires
qui les concernent ».

The CSM is included here only to mark the boundary: **it disciplines judges and prosecutors,
not police.** France's police-oversight architecture and its judicial-discipline architecture
are different systems, and merging them on an "oversight" page would be the error this
platform exists to avoid.

## 7. What the French evidence establishes about the architecture

1. **France has no external, police-specific complaints or investigation body.** The bodies
   with police-specific mandates (IGPN, IGGN) are internal. The bodies that are external
   (Défenseur des droits, CGLPL) have general mandates that reach police among others. Nothing
   in the corpus occupies the intersection.
2. **A member of the public has more than one route, and they are not equivalent.** A
   _signalement_ to the IGPN platform is expressly not a complaint. A _plainte_ is a criminal
   complaint. A _saisine_ of the Défenseur des droits is free and open to victims and
   witnesses and leads to recommendations rather than sanctions. Municipal police are a
   separate matter again: the ministry directs reports about a _policier municipal_ to the
   mairie of the place concerned.
3. **Both national forces share one deontology code.** Code de la sécurité intérieure,
   art. R434-1 (in force 1 January 2014): « Les dispositions du présent chapitre constituent le
   code de déontologie de la police nationale et de la gendarmerie nationale pour l'exécution de
   leurs missions de sécurité intérieure. » Article R434-2 adds that the two forces « sont
   soumises à des règles déontologiques communes et à des règles propres à chacune d'elles » — a
   civil-status force and an armed force held to one shared standard plus their own.
4. **Internal position does not mean weak powers, and external position does not mean strong
   ones.** The internal IGPN can open a criminal investigation on its own initiative; the
   external, constitutionally established Défenseur des droits cannot investigate a crime at
   all and cannot impose a sanction. Any page that treats "external" as a proxy for "more
   powerful" would get France exactly backwards.

Point 4 is the finding of this wave. `oversightPosture` records **position**, and France is
the clearest demonstration in the corpus of why that field was deliberately made weaker than
independence.

## 8. Uncertainty — recorded, not resolved

- Whether the IGPN carries an **express deontology mandate** is not established by the
  instruments read. The IGGN's does say so in terms; the IGPN's décret and arrêté do not. This
  may be an artefact of which instruments were read, and it is stated as unestablished rather
  than as an absence.
- The article numbers of the loi organique governing referral to the procureur and
  observations before courts could not be pinned to a single number across two independent
  reads, so those two powers are described without an article number. The powers themselves
  were confirmed twice.
- The **effectiveness** of none of these bodies is addressed. No caseload, substantiation
  rate, outcome or public-confidence figure was gathered or used, and none may be inferred
  from anything above.
- The **Conseil supérieur de la magistrature** and the ordinary courts' review of police
  action were not researched beyond the constitutional text cited.
