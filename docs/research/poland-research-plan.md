# Poland — research plan

Poland is country 5 of Batch B and the batch's honesty-critical case: the governance of its
judiciary is a live, contested question. The research discipline was therefore doubled — every
load-bearing fact from primary statute, every contested assessment attributed to a dated official or
EU source, and nothing characterised in the site's own voice.

## Questions the pilot had to answer

1. **Unitary or devolved?** — Constitution Art. 3: unitary. Territorial self-government holds no
   justice competence. → one `pl` record, all functions `own`, no region record.
2. **Where does the prosecution sit?** — the Prokuratura has no dedicated constitutional basis; the
   Law on the Prosecutor's Office 2016 (Art. 1 § 2) makes the office of Prosecutor-General held by
   the Minister of Justice — a personal union. The most executive-fused prosecution on the site.
3. **How many top courts?** — three separate bodies: the Supreme Court (Art. 183), a separate
   supreme administrative court (Art. 184 — "Chief Administrative Court" in the official
   translation), and the Constitutional Tribunal (Arts. 188–190).
4. **Police, investigation, prisons?** — Policja (single national force under the interior minister,
   1990 Act); prosecutor directs the pre-trial phase (KPK Arts. 298, 311, 326); Prison Service under
   the Minister of Justice (2010 Act).
5. **What is the contested material, and how is it handled?** — two enacted measures (the 2016
   PG–MoJ union; the 2017 change to how the KRS's judicial members are chosen) are stated as facts
   with statutory citations; the contested _assessment_ is carried only by attribution to the CJEU
   judgment in C-204/21 (5 June 2023) and the Commission's 2025 Rule of Law Report (8 July 2025).
   Proposed-but-not-enacted measures (PG–MoJ separation; the 2024 KRS re-reform referred to the
   Constitutional Tribunal) are labelled as proposed. News-media-only developments are excluded.
6. **Prison numbers?** — SPACE I 2024: 73,822 / 86,109 / density 85.7 (under capacity). The high
   per-100k rate is kept out of the claim, because a rate invites the cross-country comparison SPACE
   I warns against.

## Sources targeted (all primary or official/EU)

Constitutional Tribunal (Constitution EN); the Sejm ELI service for four primary statutes (Law on
the Prosecutor's Office, Law on the Police, Code of Criminal Procedure, the 2017 KRS amendment); the
Law on the Prison Service; the CJEU C-204/21 judgment (via the FRA reference); the Commission's 2025
Rule of Law Report; and the shared CoE SPACE I 2024.

## Neutrality rules applied

- Enacted vs. proposed kept strictly distinct.
- Every contested assessment attributed to a named, dated source; no site-voice characterisation.
- No partisan language — a test scans all reader-facing Poland prose and fails on a forbidden-terms
  list (captured, backsliding, authoritarian, party names, purge, crisis, …).
- Honest naming (NSA both renderings) and honest gaps (common-court tier names not asserted;
  news-media developments excluded).

## Deferrals

Forensics and border/customs (safety-sensitive, not researched to standard), history and timeline
(need scholarly per-milestone sources, and neutrality demands especially careful sourcing). All four
deferred with reasons; no routes.
