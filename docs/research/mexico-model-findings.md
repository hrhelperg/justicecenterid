# Mexico — model findings

What Mexico added to the comparative picture, and what it reused.

## 1. A fully dual federation — every function replicated at both tiers

India split the four functions across tiers (police/prosecution/prisons State; courts integrated).
Mexico goes further: **all four functions are duplicated federally and in each of 32 states** — there
are federal courts and state judiciaries, a federal prosecution and state ones, federal/state/
municipal police, and federal and state prisons. So the federal record carries every function as
`shared`, and the state records carry every function as `own`. This is the US pattern (dual courts,
dual police, dual prisons), and Mexico is modelled the same way — a federal record plus two
representative state records (Jalisco, Nuevo León). No new schema.

## 2. High harmonisation on top of full duality — a new combination

What makes Mexico distinct from the US-style dual federation is the degree of harmonisation over the
duplicated institutions: a **single national code** of criminal procedure and sentence execution
(Art. 73-XXI-c) governs both orders, the criminal process is **accusatory and oral nationwide**
(Art. 20, completed 2016), and since 2024 a **uniform elected-judiciary model** is imposed on the
states (Art. 116-III). So the institutions are dual but the _rules_ are national. This is a genuinely
new point on the federal spectrum — full institutional duality with near-uniform procedure — and it
is captured by pairing `shared`/`own` scopes with prose.

## 3. The batch's second Poland-class neutrality case

The 2024 judicial reform (popular election of judges) is politically contested and was handled with
the full neutrality discipline first used for Poland: enacted facts stated with the gazette citation
and dates; every assessment attributed to a named, dated intergovernmental source (the IACHR press
release 213/2024; the OAS Electoral Observation Mission's June 2025 report, including its ~13%
turnout finding and its explicit "does not recommend replicating" conclusion); and a **mechanical
neutrality test** scanning all reader-facing Mexico prose for partisan terms. Poland was a domestic-
statute + CJEU/EC case; Mexico is a constitutional-reform + IACHR/OAS case — the same discipline,
different source ecosystem.

## 4. An autonomous prosecution that also directs the investigation

Mexico's federal Fiscalía General is constitutionally autonomous (Art. 102-A, since 2018) — placing
it near Portugal on the independence spectrum — but, unlike the common-law systems, the prosecutor
**directs the investigation** (police under its "conduct and command", Art. 21). Autonomous _and_
investigation-directing is a combination the site had not yet shown.

## 5. Currency discipline again

As with Indonesia, the World Prison Brief figures had updated between research and authoring (265,874
→ 268,245), and its ministry label was stale (a body dissolved in 2018). Both were caught by the
hand-confirmation rule and handled by using the current value and naming the stale one.

## Net schema change

**None.** Mexico reused the `federal`/`state` levels, the representative-subset pattern, and the
neutrality discipline; the full-duality-plus-harmonisation finding lives in the scope values, prose
and tests.
