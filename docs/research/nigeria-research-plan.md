# Nigeria — research plan

Nigeria is country 10 of 10 in Batch C, and the batch's most structurally distinctive federation: a
country that decentralises courts, prosecution and criminal law to the states while keeping police
and prisons federal — the inverse of the other federations on the site.

## Questions the pilot had to answer

1. **Federal, and which tier runs what?** — a federation (36 states + FCT). The Second Schedule
   splits legislative power into an Exclusive List (federal), a Concurrent List (both) and residual
   matters (states, s 4(7)). Mapping the four functions: **police federal** (item 45, s 214(1) bars
   any other force), **prisons federal** (item 48), **courts at both levels** (s 6(5)), **prosecution
   dual** (AGF s 174 / State AG s 211), **criminal law residual/state** (Criminal Code, Penal Code,
   Sharia penal codes).
2. **What is the apex and the review model?** — a single federal Supreme Court (s 230), whose
   determinations are final (s 235); constitutional review is diffuse (any superior court of record;
   no separate constitutional court).
3. **Where does prosecution sit?** — inside the executive: the Attorney-General of the Federation is
   a Cabinet Minister and Chief Law Officer (s 150), with a mirror State Attorney-General in each
   state (s 195/211) — not a structurally independent prosecution.
4. **Police and prisons?** — one federal Nigeria Police Force (s 214–215), no state police; a federal
   Nigerian Correctional Service, no state prisons.
5. **The Sharia question (the sub-national criminal-law variant)** — from 1999 to 2001 twelve
   northern states adopted Sharia penal codes (attributed to a 2004 Human Rights Watch report). Stated
   by dated attribution; the contested human-rights assessment is not adopted as the site's own view.
6. **Oversight?** — a federal National Judicial Council (which unifies the two-tier judiciary), the
   National Human Rights Commission, the Public Complaints Commission (ombudsman), and police
   oversight split between the Police Service Commission and the Nigeria Police Council.
7. **Prisons and numbers?** — World Prison Brief: population 81,902 (22 Jun 2026) at a rate of ~34 (a
   low rate), capacity 65,035 and occupancy 125.6% (both 30 Jun 2025) — reported with the two dates
   kept distinct.

## Why a federal record + two state records (the contrast that matters)

Nigeria is the batch's demonstration that a federation can decentralise **courts, prosecution and
criminal law** while **centralising police and prisons** — the mirror image of India, Mexico,
Argentina and the US, where the sub-national tier runs its own police and prisons. To make that
inverse split legible, two illustrative state records are modelled — **Kano** (a northern
Sharia-penal-code state) and **Lagos** (a southern common-law state) — each running courts,
prosecution and criminal law (`own`) but with policing and corrections `none` (federal). This keeps
the batch's modelling rule intact: a record exists where a tier does justice work.

## Honesty points

- The Sharia penal codes and the pending state-police bill are stated by dated attribution, not as
  settled or adjudicated fact; the safeguards debate is attributed to both sides.
- Current office-holders are not named; the EFCC/ICPC/ACJA/NCoS statutes and the PCC statute are
  named from secondary sources without re-fetching.
- The two prison reference dates are kept distinct; a low imprisonment rate is explicitly not treated
  as a claim about conditions.

## Sources targeted

The 1999 Constitution (full official PDF, Second Schedule read verbatim), the World Prison Brief, a
2004 Human Rights Watch report (Sharia dating), and the National Assembly Library Trust Fund
(state-police bill status).

## Model discipline

No new schema. The inverse-federation split is carried by the federal record's scopes (police and
prisons `own`, courts/prosecution/legal-system `shared`) and the two state records' scopes
(courts/prosecution/legal-system `own`, policing/corrections `none`); the Sharia variant and the
pending reform live in prose and the tests.
