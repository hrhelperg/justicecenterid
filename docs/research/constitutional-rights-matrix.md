# Comparative rights matrix — Wave 21

Every cell was read from the official publisher's own text. Status vocabulary is the brief's:
**ESTABLISHED** (primary text read and confirmed), **PARTIALLY ESTABLISHED** (text read, but the
proposition needs material not researched here), **NOT ESTABLISHED** (text read and it does not
say this), **NOT RESEARCHED** (not looked at — never rendered as "no").

The distinction between NOT ESTABLISHED and NOT RESEARCHED is load-bearing. "The Grundgesetz
contains no privilege against self-incrimination" is NOT ESTABLISHED — an exhaustive search of the
authoritative German text returned nothing. "France requires a judge to authorise a home search"
is NOT RESEARCHED — the Code de procédure pénale was not read for this wave.

a free/open and democratic society" appears in three of them (Canada, South Africa, Kenya) and
does different work in each: Canada lists no factors, South Africa lists five, Kenya lists five
and then adds a validity rule, a core-content bar and an express burden of proof.

**Finding.** There is no universal proportionality test, and the strongest evidence for that is
that the two treaties most often cited for the doctrine never use the word. Switzerland shows the
second finding: proportionality is not only a rights doctrine — Art. 5(2) constrains state action
that limits no right at all.

assembly-type rights, both giving liberty and privacy to everyone). One ties them to citizenship
much more widely (Ireland). One ties them to residence (Brazil). Three switch holder mid-instrument
(Japan, the United States, South Africa). The word "citizen" is not a safe default anywhere.

**Judicial interpretation of these words was NOT RESEARCHED** and no page claims that the textual
holder is the operative holder.
