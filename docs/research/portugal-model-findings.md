# Portugal — model findings

What Portugal added to the comparative picture, and what it reused. The rule throughout: reuse an
existing model where the facts fit, mint nothing new without evidence.

## 1. A prosecution autonomous in two directions

Portugal's _Ministério Público_ is the clearest instance so far of a prosecution that is **neither**
part of the judiciary **nor** under a minister. Constitution Art. 219 gives it "its own statute and
autonomy". This sits between two models already on the site:

- **Italy** — prosecutors are _magistrati_ **inside** the independent judiciary.
- **Denmark / Netherlands** — the prosecution service sits **under** the justice ministry.

Portugal is a third position: a self-standing autonomous body, governed by its own Prosecutor
General's Office and High Council, constrained by the principle of legality. No new schema was
needed — `prosecutionScope: 'own'` at country level plus prose carries it — but it is a genuinely
distinct institutional design, and the prose says so explicitly rather than forcing Portugal into
the Italian or the Danish box.

## 2. Four top courts + diffuse review (the double constitutional-review pattern)

Portugal both (a) has a **dedicated** Constitutional Court and (b) requires **every** court to
disapply unconstitutional norms (Art. 204). Combined with the Supreme Court of Justice, the Supreme
Administrative Court and the Court of Auditors, that is four top courts alongside diffuse review.
This mirrors the "concentrated + diffuse" combination and reuses existing description patterns; it
is recorded as a fact, not padded into a fifth court.

## 3. The two-phase, magistrate-directed investigation

The _inquérito_ (prosecution-directed) followed by the optional _instrução_ (investigating-judge-led)
is described from the Code of Criminal Procedure (Arts. 263, 17). It reuses the general
"magistrate-directed investigation" frame without importing Brazilian vocabulary, even though the
two systems share terms.

## 4. Unitary-with-autonomous-regions, justice fully national — the Italy result, not the Åland result

Like Italy, Portugal is decentralised (two autonomous regions) but **centralised for justice**: the
regions hold no competence over courts, prosecution, police or prisons. So — as with Italy — a single
country-level record with all functions `own` and **no** region record. This is the deliberate
counter-example to Finland's Åland, which **does** hold policing competence and therefore earned its
own `autonomous-community`-level record. The contrast is stated in prose on both the Italy and
Portugal pages.

The only region-adjacent element is a regional section of the national Court of Auditors
(Art. 214(4)) — a decentralised chamber of a national court, not devolved competence — and it is
described as such, not as a regional justice institution.

## 5. Restricted claim: just under capacity

Portugal's SPACE I density is **96.3** — the system as a whole held slightly **fewer** people than
its stated capacity on the reference date. This is the mirror image of Italy's 118.1 (over capacity)
and is stated with the same three limitations (single-day snapshot, national aggregate, no
cross-country comparison). Recording an under-capacity figure with the same rigour as an
over-capacity one is part of the honesty discipline: the number is reported because it is sourced,
not because it flatters or damns.

## Net schema change

**None.** Portugal reused existing levels, scopes and claim types. Its distinctiveness lives in
prose and in the tests, exactly as the "automate structure, not truth" principle intends.
