Research findings from Knowledge Expansion Wave 33. Two guides, three new sources, two systems:
the Netherlands and England and Wales.

The genuinely new and genuinely safe ground is the other half of the subject: not what a police
vehicle can do, but **what the law permits its driver to do, and who has to authorise it.**

## The exemption is narrower and more conditional than people assume

**The Netherlands publishes its exemption as a list.** It covers proceeding through a red light
("doorrijden bij een rood verkeerslicht"), driving on the hard shoulder ("rijden over de
vluchtstrook"), driving where others may not — tram lanes, bus lanes, pavements — overtaking across
a solid line ("inhalen over een doorgetrokken streep"), and stopping or parking where that is
prohibited to others.

Publishing it as named categories is itself a limit: it tells a reader what is covered, which also
tells them what is not.

**Three conditions attach**, and the first does most of the work:

1. "de verkeersveiligheid mag niet in gevaar komen" — road safety must not be endangered
2. "het gebruik van de vrijstelling is gezien de situatie noodzakelijk" — use must be necessary
3. compliance with the police traffic guideline

An exemption that may not endanger road safety is not permission to create danger. It is permission
to depart from a rule while remaining answerable for the outcome.

**England and Wales, in the section read, is about speed alone**, and is conditional on the same
kind of test:

> No statutory provision imposing a speed limit on motor vehicles shall apply to any vehicle on an
> occasion when it is being used for fire and rescue authority, for ambulance purposes or police
> purposes, if the observance of that provision would be likely to hinder the use of the vehicle for
> the purpose for which it is being used on that occasion.

Two things follow. The exemption **attaches to the occasion, not permanently to the vehicle** — a
police car is not exempt for being a police car. And a later subsection makes prescribed high-speed
driving instruction a condition for National Crime Agency purposes, so where a system attaches
training to an exemption, the exemption belongs to a qualified driver rather than to a role.

**What is deliberately not concluded:** that speed is the only English exemption. One section was
read. Whether other provisions cover red lights, bus lanes or anything else was not researched, and
absence of research is not a finding.

## The signals are not the driver's decision

> De politie mag uitsluitend met toestemming van de meldkamer gebruikmaken van optische en
> geluidssignalen.

_Uitsluitend_ — only. Not normally, not where practicable. The authorisation is the condition of
use, which is a stronger statement than most services make about anything a crew does in a moving
vehicle.

Three consequences the same page states:

- **The permission is bounded.** It ends once other emergency services have already arrived.
- **Urgency and signals are separable.** In some situations officers respond urgently _without_
  them. Reading the absence of lights as evidence that little is happening gets it backwards.
- **Permission is not a defence.** "Dit betekent niet dat politieagenten roekeloos mogen rijden, of
  dat ze het overige verkeer in gevaar mogen brengen." A member of the public may complain about
  police driving.

Putting the authorisation in the control room also creates a record of a decision that would
otherwise exist only in a driver's memory — and it is the same room Wave 29 described taking the
call and sending the unit.

## Why an exemption is an accountability question

It is one of the few police powers exercised against people who are not suspected of anything. The
pedestrian at the crossing has no involvement in the emergency and carries the whole of the
transferred risk. That is why the conditions are the substantive part of the arrangement.
