# Police equipment — commercial boundary and future opportunity (INTERNAL)

**Internal research. Nothing here is published**, and a test asserts it: monetisation vocabulary
appears in no published guide, no profession record, no country module and no source note.

Wave 26 adds **no commerce of any kind** — no product, listing, affiliate link, price, supplier,
sponsorship or partnership. This records where a future commercial layer could and could not sit.

## The rule that governs everything

**Commercial opportunity must never determine a factual conclusion — and in this wave it must not
determine what is described at all.** Equipment is the first subject where the temptation is not to
shade a conclusion but to add detail that has no editorial purpose and obvious commercial value:
specifications, comparisons, protection levels, capability. Every one of those is absent, and the
absence is tested.

## The hard boundary, restated

| Category                                                        | Informational coverage                               | Commerce                          | Procurement / configuration / use |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Firearms, ammunition, components, silencers                     | May note the category exists where a source names it | **NEVER**                         | **NEVER**                         |
| Batons, restraints, tasers, incapacitant spray                  | Same                                                 | **NEVER**                         | **NEVER**                         |
| Breaching tools, tactical weapons                               | Same                                                 | **NEVER**                         | **NEVER**                         |
| Surveillance, interception, counter-surveillance equipment      | Same                                                 | **NEVER**                         | **NEVER**                         |
| Body armour                                                     | Institutional / standards level only                 | **NEVER**                         | **NEVER**                         |
| Uniforms, insignia, identification                              | Institutional description                            | **NEVER** — impersonation risk    | **NEVER**                         |
| Notebooks, stationery, bags, general apparel, footwear, torches | Institutional description                            | Possible later, clearly separated | No configuration guidance         |

**Two categories that look benign are excluded from commerce anyway.** Uniforms and identification
items, because anything that helps someone assemble the appearance of a police officer is an
impersonation risk regardless of the item's legal status. And body armour, because it sits under a
safety standards regime where a purchasing recommendation would carry consequences this platform
cannot stand behind.

The pattern: **the only safe commercial surface is the equipment that is not police equipment** —
notebooks, bags, general apparel, books. Anything specific to policing carries either a misuse
surface or an impersonation surface, and usually both.

## Standing rules, carried forward and extended

1. Paid placement is labelled, always and visibly.
2. Sponsorship never alters a factual ranking or conclusion.
3. An affiliate relationship never determines an editorial conclusion.
4. Official sources remain authoritative regardless of any commercial relationship.
5. A commercial partner cannot purchase a factual claim.
6. No provider can buy status; no such status exists.
7. Product monetisation stays structurally separate from institutional facts.
8. No commercial relationship with a recruiting authority whose requirements are described.
9. **New in Wave 26:** no commercial relationship with any manufacturer or supplier of police
   equipment, of any category, regulated or not — and no page may name one. A test asserts that no
   manufacturer or brand appears anywhere in the corpus.
