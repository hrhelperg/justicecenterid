# Police oversight institutions — plan and classification

**Status:** 2 institution types + 1 guide published, 2026-08-10.

## 1. The finding that set the scope

The brief asked for approximately 6–10 institution routes "if the evidence supports them",
and set a source standard of statutes, official oversight-body sites and official government
descriptions.

The corpus was searched before anything was written. It holds:

- **six national police-oversight bodies**, each verified in an earlier wave — Ireland
  (Fiosrú), New Zealand (IPCA), South Africa (IPID), Norway (Spesialenheten), Czechia (GIBS),
  Denmark (Police Complaints Authority);
- **five ombuds / rights institutions** — Norway, Sweden, Austria, Czechia, Denmark, plus
  Kenya's Article 59 commissions;
- **no internal-affairs source, and no police-inspectorate source, at all.**

That last point is decisive rather than incidental. Searching for one returns nothing: the
only "Inspectorate" in the registry is Ireland's Garda Síochána Inspectorate, dissolved on
2 April 2025, and every "Inspector-General" refers to a police chief rather than an oversight
body.

**Two institution types are therefore published, not six to ten.** The brief's own
instruction — _do not force publication merely to hit the target_ — is the operative one.

## 2. The corpus did not match the brief's expected countries

The brief named eleven countries as likely evidence: Ireland, the UK, France, Germany, the
United States, Canada, Australia, Spain, Switzerland, Japan, Kenya, Nigeria, South Africa.

Of those, only **Ireland, South Africa, Kenya and Nigeria** carry oversight sourcing. There
is no IOPC source, no IGPN or IGGN source, no Défenseur des droits source, no CRCC source, no
US civilian-review or inspector-general source, and no German, Swiss, Spanish or Australian
oversight source.

The countries that _do_ carry it — New Zealand, Norway, Czechia, Denmark, Sweden, Austria —
were not on the list.

**The pages follow the corpus.** Writing about the IOPC, IGPN or US civilian review boards
would have meant asserting institutional facts with no verified source, which is the failure
every wave since Wave 2 has been built to prevent.

## 4. The guide

One new law-enforcement guide: **`/law-enforcement/who-investigates-police`**.

The brief asked whether it could be made genuinely comparative, and said to defer it if it
became a list of countries without synthesis. It is comparative: five arrangements are
distinguished — the service investigates itself, another police service investigates, an
external statutory body investigates, an external body investigates _and prosecutes_, or a
prosecutor directs — with four sourced country cases and a sourced counterexample.

Two candidates from Parts H and J were **deferred**:

- `internal-vs-external-police-oversight` — `how-police-are-held-to-account` already
  distinguishes internal control from external mechanisms. A second page would restate it.
- `how-police-complaints-are-investigated` — no process source in the corpus, and the risk of
  drifting into jurisdiction-specific procedural advice is exactly what Part J warns about.

## 5. Independence, and what is claimed instead

Part E is the most important constraint in this brief. Independence is claimed **only** where
a source establishes a basis for it:

| Body                      | Basis in the source                                                                                             |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| New Zealand IPCA          | "set up by Parliament", "not part of the NZ Police", "under law we are fully independent", established 1989     |
| South Africa IPID         | Independent Police Investigative Directorate Act 1 of 2011                                                      |
| Czechia GIBS              | Act No. 341/2011 Coll.                                                                                          |
| Denmark                   | Act no. 404 of 21 April 2010; independent of both police and prosecution; council chaired by a High Court judge |
| Norway Spesialenheten     | outside the ordinary police and prosecution chain                                                               |
| Sweden JO                 | Instrument of Government Ch. 13 Art. 6, appointed by the Riksdag                                                |
| Austria Volksanwaltschaft | Article 148a B-VG                                                                                               |

A new typed field, `oversightPosture`, records **position** — inside or outside the
organisation examined. It is deliberately weaker than independence and is the claim that can
be made about every body here. No comparative or superlative independence language appears
anywhere, and a test forbids it.

## 6. Powers, and refusing to flatten them

The pages state repeatedly that the shared label is the weakest thing these bodies have in
common:

- **Norway** — investigates _and prosecutes_ police-related crime.
- **South Africa** — confined by statute to deaths, firearm discharges, and allegations of
  torture, assault or corruption.
- **Czechia** — covers police, prison service _and_ customs, and opens prosecutions.
- **New Zealand** — watches and investigates; criminal decisions rest elsewhere.
- **Denmark** — criminal cases, conduct complaints, deaths and serious injuries.
- **Ireland** — receives and investigates complaints about Garda personnel.

"Investigating is not the same as recommending; recommending is not the same as disciplining;
and disciplining is not the same as prosecuting" is stated on the page and asserted by test.

## 7. Temporal integrity

Ireland exercises the rule the brief set. The `ScheduledChange`
`ie-policing-oversight-reform-2025` was already modelled with `effectiveOn: 2025-04-02`; the
new page names **Fiosrú as current** and **GSOC as historical from that date**, and a test
asserts the page's date matches the modelled change.

**No new ScheduledChange was needed.** The reform was already recorded, which is the system
working as designed.

## 8. Restricted claims

**None added.** No effectiveness, public-confidence, substantiation-rate, corruption or
misconduct-prevalence claim appears. The pages state explicitly that the existence of an
independent body is a fact about institutional design and not about outcomes — the exact
inference Part R forbids.
