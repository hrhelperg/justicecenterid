## Primary vs secondary — stated plainly

- **Primary (fetched):** the Constitution (the entire federalism tier allocation — that police,
  prosecution and prisons are State subjects — is verbatim from the primary text), the Press
  Information Bureau release, and the World Prison Brief.
- **Secondary (described, flagged):** the CBI (Delhi Special Police Establishment Act 1946) and NIA
  (NIA Act 2008); the Directorate of Prosecution provision (BNSS s. 20) and the police-investigation
  provisions (BNSS s. 175); the NHRC (Protection of Human Rights Act 1993); the collegium/NJAC
  history (the 2015 judgment); and the State Police Complaints Authorities (Prakash Singh v. Union of
  India, 2006). These are consistent with reputable sources but were not primary-fetched, and the
  dossier says so.

## Access notes

The Ministry of Home Affairs "three new criminal laws" page, the CBI site, and the NIA site were
bot-walled, JavaScript-rendered or unavailable, so the federal-agency detail rests on secondary
sources. A contested-but-decided litigation point (a challenge to BNSS s. 20(2)(a), dismissed by the
Supreme Court in February 2026) was left out as not load-bearing for the institutional structure.

## Model note

India is the batch's first genuine federal case needing **sub-national jurisdiction records**. It is
modelled as a Union record plus three illustrative State records (Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Uttar
Pradesh) — the same "federal record + representative subset" pattern used for Germany and the United
States. The 28 States and the Union Territories are not all modelled; UTs differ.
