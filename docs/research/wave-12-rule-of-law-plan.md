Wave 12 must own **different reader questions**. The definitions of the rule of law and of due
process are taken; what is not taken is the comparative constitutional material showing how
systems actually implement these ideas, and under what names.

## Jurisdictional diversity — a deliberate correction

Earlier waves leaned heavily on Germany because its code is accessible and precise. This wave was
built on **four** constitutional systems reached from primary text, three of them outside the
German-French axis the corpus over-uses.

| Jurisdiction | Provisions read | Establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Germany** (Grundgesetz) | Art. 3(1), 19(4), 20(2)–(3), 103(1)–(3) | Equality before the law; recourse to the courts against public authority; the executive and judiciary bound by law; the right to be heard; legality and non-retroactivity of punishment; ne bis in idem |
| **South Africa** (Constitution 1996) | ss. 9(1), 33, 34, 35(2) | Equality before the law **and** equal protection; administrative action that is lawful, reasonable and procedurally fair, with written reasons; access to courts; counsel at state expense where substantial injustice would otherwise result; detention conditions consistent with human dignity |
| **Kenya** (Constitution 2010) | Arts. 10, 48, 50, 159 | The rule of law as a binding national value; a constitutional access-to-justice guarantee including a **fee constraint**; fair hearing before a court or other independent tribunal; judicial authority derived from the people, with ADR promoted and technicality discouraged |
| **Brazil** (CF/88) | Art. 5º XXXV, XXXVI, XXXVII, XXXIX, XL, LIV, LV, LVI, LVII, LXXV, LXXVIII | Inafastabilidade — no law may exclude injury or threat to a right from judicial appraisal; protection of vested rights and res judicata; no exceptional tribunals; no crime without prior law; non-retroactivity save to benefit the accused; **devido processo legal**; contraditório e ampla defesa; exclusion of unlawfully obtained evidence; guilt only on final conviction; indemnity for judicial error; reasonable duration |

South Africa's text was taken from the clean digital PDF published by the Department of Justice
and corroborated against gov.za's chapter pages. **The gov.za scanned PDF of the whole Act was
rejected as a source**: it is an OCR of a printed gazette and contains visible corruption
("Lefcre a court", "icdepi:ndent"). Nothing is quoted from it.

## The due-process finding — better than the brief anticipated

The brief instructed that the American constitutional term must not be universalised. Research
produced a more interesting result than a simple prohibition.

**The term did travel.** Article 5º LIV of Brazil's Constitution provides that no one shall be
deprived of liberty or property "sem o devido processo legal" — a direct Portuguese rendering.
So "due process" is not a purely American expression, and a page saying so would be wrong.

**The concept cluster did not travel intact.** Brazil pairs LIV with LV, guaranteeing
"o contraditório e ampla defesa" — the adversarial principle and full defence — which the American
formula does not name. And three of the four systems here reach the same ground under different
vocabulary that is not a translation of it:

- Germany: **rechtliches Gehör**, a claim to be heard before a court (Art. 103(1))
- South Africa: administrative action that is **procedurally fair**, plus a **fair public hearing** (ss. 33, 34)
- Kenya: a **fair hearing** before a court or other independent and impartial tribunal (Art. 50)

The honest comparative statement is therefore neither "due process is universal" nor "due process
is American". It is that one term spread to some systems, that others reached comparable
guarantees under their own names, and that the guarantees bundled under each name differ.

## Proportionality — deferred

The brief flagged proportionality as high-risk. Research confirmed the risk: none of the four
constitutions read for this wave states a proportionality test in terms, and the German
proportionality doctrine is judicial rather than textual. Writing a comparative proportionality
page would have required constitutional-court jurisprudence that was not read. **Deferred**, and
recorded as the clearest single research opportunity for a later wave.

## The Cicero position — preserved, not repeated

Wave 9 established the full sentence at *Pro Cluentio* §146 and the correction that the popular
truncation drops the clauses about magistrates and judges. `/courts/why-courts-matter` owns that
material. Wave 12 does not restate it, does not re-quote the passage, and links to the courts page
where the point is relevant. A test asserts the truncated form appears nowhere.
