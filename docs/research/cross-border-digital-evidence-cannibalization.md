| Layer                                             | Owner       | Question                                                                                          |

| Constitutional right and its limit                | Wave 21     | _Why_ the state's reach is bounded                                                                |
| Domestic statutory authority over digital objects | Wave 22     | _What_ authority exists, authorised by whom                                                       |
| **Cross-border cooperation and jurisdiction**     | **Wave 23** | _What happens when the authority is here and the data, the provider or the evidence is elsewhere_ |
| Forensic science as evidence                      | Wave 16     | What an examination establishes                                                                   |
| Review and remedy                                 | Wave 19     | What follows when authority was exceeded                                                          |

Wave 23's territory was signposted by Wave 22's own record. The corpus's Convention source is
titled **"Articles 14–21"** and its STATUS line records that the Second Additional Protocol "was
located but NOT read for this wave." Every one of Wave 22's seven Convention citations is an
Article from Chapter II Section 2 — the _domestic_ powers. **Chapter III, Articles 23 to 35, is
untouched**, and so is every other cooperation instrument.

## The distinction that decided the shape

Two candidates looked like duplicates of Wave 22 and are not, and getting that right determined
the wave. `cross-border-preservation-and-disclosure` survives because Convention Art. 29 changes
the _legal_ question, not just the geography: dual criminality is expressly excluded for
preservation and may be reserved for disclosure, so the two steps can have different answers for
the same data. `data-categories-across-instruments` survives because the EU defines four categories
where the Convention defines three, so a reader who learned the Wave 22 categories would be wrong
about the EU instrument. In both cases the test is Part H's: the cross-border mechanism changes the
legal question. Where it did not — a foreign provider being ordered under domestic law — the
candidate was rejected.
