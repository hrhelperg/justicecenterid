Five systems, researched to content-confirmed Tier 1 sources between 5 and 6 September 2026.
Every cell is one of: **ESTABLISHED**, **PARTIALLY ESTABLISHED**, **NOT ESTABLISHED**,
**NOT RESEARCHED**, **VARIABLE WITHIN JURISDICTION**. Missing evidence is never converted to "no".

## 5. What is trained (United States, 2022 census — the only system with published subject hours)

Average hours of instruction, from the BJS Census of Law Enforcement Training Academies, 2022:

| Subject                         | Average hours |
| ------------------------------- | ------------- |
| Firearms skills                 | 73            |
| Defensive tactics               | 64            |
| Health and fitness              | 56            |
| Criminal and constitutional law | 51            |
| Traffic law                     | 26            |
| Juvenile justice law            | 10            |

Over 97% of recruits were instructed in legal subjects. Over 80% were trained in at least one
community-related topic. 97% of academies required an average of 16 skill or proficiency
assessments; 95% required written and physical fitness assessments.

**These are subject headings and hour counts from a government census.** Nothing in this wave
describes how any technique is performed.

## 7. The finding that reorganises the comparison

**The Netherlands offers a bachelor Rechercheur — an entry route directly into investigative work.**
Alongside it sit bachelor Politiekunde Politieagent, bachelor Politiekunde Wijkagent (community
officer) and bachelor Politieleider (police leader): four distinct three-year hbo bachelors, plus
the two-year mbo-4 route.

In systems where investigation is reached after service in uniform, "detective" is something a
serving officer becomes. In the Netherlands it is a programme a person can be admitted to. The word
names an occupational entry route in one system and a later assignment in another, which is why the
rank/role/specialisation distinction (Part R) needs its own page rather than a paragraph.
