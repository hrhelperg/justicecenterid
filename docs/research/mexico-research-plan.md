# Mexico — research plan

Mexico is country 7 of Batch C and carries two hard demands at once: a genuine dual-tier federation
across all four functions, and a politically charged 2024 judicial reform that must be handled with
Poland-class neutrality.

## Questions the pilot had to answer

1. **Federal or unitary, and which tier runs what?** — a federation (Art. 40) with residual powers to
   the states (Art. 124). Unlike India, ALL FOUR functions are dual: federal courts + 32 state
   judiciaries; the autonomous federal Fiscalía + 32 state fiscalías; federal Guardia Nacional +
   state + municipal police (Art. 21 shared security); federal + state prisons (Art. 18). → a federal
   record + representative state records.
2. **What holds it together?** — a single national code of criminal procedure and sentence execution
   (Art. 73-XXI-c), an accusatory oral process nationwide since 2016 (Art. 20), and — since 2024 — a
   uniform elected-judiciary model imposed on the states (Art. 116-III).
3. **What is the apex and review model?** — the Supreme Court (nine Ministers since 2024) is the
   constitutional apex; no separate constitutional court; amparo + actions of unconstitutionality.
4. **Where does prosecution sit?** — the federal Fiscalía General is constitutionally autonomous
   (Art. 102-A, since 2018); the prosecutor directs the investigation, police under its command
   (Art. 21).
5. **The 2024 judicial reform?** — enacted (DOF 15 Sept 2024): judges elected by popular vote
   (Art. 96), imposed on states (Art. 116-III), SCJN reduced to 9; first elections 1 June 2025, new
   Supreme Court installed 1 Sept 2025 (first Indigenous President), second round 2027.
6. **Prisons and numbers?** — a dual system; World Prison Brief: 268,245 at 31 May 2026 against
   capacity 227,658 — 117.8%, above capacity, with about 43% in pre-trial detention.

## The neutrality demand (contested reform)

The 2024 judicial reform is the batch's second Poland-class case. Enacted facts are stated with the
gazette citation and dates; every assessment is attributed to a named, dated intergovernmental source
— the IACHR press release 213/2024 and the OAS Electoral Observation Mission — and the site adds no
view of its own. A test scans all reader-facing Mexico prose and fails on partisan terms.

## Corrections applied

The Art. 21 investigation clause is paraphrased (its current text expanded since the brief's stale
quote), and the Guardia Nacional amended-article list is not enumerated (the brief's list was
incomplete).

## Sources targeted

The 1917 Constitution (Supreme Court per-article texts + current-text mirror), the official gazette
for the reform, the IACHR and OAS for the assessments, and the World Prison Brief.

## Model discipline

Reuse of the `federal`/`state` levels and the representative-subset pattern (US/India) — no new
schema. The dual-tier fact, the accusatory model, and the neutral reform handling live in the scope
values, prose and tests.
