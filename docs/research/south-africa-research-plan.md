# South Africa — research plan

South Africa is country 9 of Batch C. It is the batch's clearest constitutional-supremacy exemplar,
and — despite being quasi-federal — a country whose justice functions are all national.

## Questions the pilot had to answer

1. **Constitutional supremacy or parliamentary sovereignty?** — supremacy: the Constitution is the
   supreme law and inconsistent law is invalid (ss 1(c), 2), so the courts can strike down statutes.
   The opposite of New Zealand.
2. **Federal, and which tier runs what?** — quasi-federal (nine provinces) but justice is national.
   Courts, prosecution, prisons and criminal law are in neither Schedule 4 nor Schedule 5, so they
   fall to the national sphere; the only provincial justice competence is police oversight (s 206(3)).
   → one country record, no sub-national record.
3. **What is the apex and review model?** — the Constitutional Court is the highest court for all
   matters since the Seventeenth Amendment (2013), with the Supreme Court of Appeal below it — an
   apex-court model of review.
4. **Where does prosecution sit?** — a single national prosecuting authority (the NPA, s 179), headed
   by the National Director appointed by the President, exercising its functions without fear/favour/
   prejudice but under the Justice Minister's "final responsibility"; with a permanent in-house
   corruption directorate (IDAC).
5. **Police and prisons?** — a single national SAPS (ss 205–207), provinces oversight-only; a national
   Department of Correctional Services.
6. **Oversight?** — unusually rich: the Chapter 9 institutions (Public Protector, Human Rights
   Commission), the Judicial Service Commission, IPID, and the Judicial Inspectorate for Correctional
   Services.
7. **Prisons and numbers?** — World Prison Brief: 168,672 at 26 Feb 2026 against capacity 107,067 —
   157.5%, well above capacity, ~37% remand.

## Why no sub-national records (the contrast that matters)

South Africa is the batch's demonstration that "quasi-federal" does not automatically mean
sub-national justice. Mexico, Argentina and India each earned sub-national records because their
states/provinces run their own courts, police and prisons. South Africa's provinces run _none_ of
the four functions — only police oversight — so a single country record is the honest model. The
schedules make this exact.

## Honesty points

- The 2025–2026 commission of inquiry into police interference and the leadership suspensions are
  left out (unresolved personnel/inquiry matters, not structure).
- IDAC's contested independence is recorded by attribution.
- An unattested nickname for the current National Director is dropped.

## Sources targeted

The 1996 Constitution (gov.za), the IPID Act, the NPA account of IDAC, and the World Prison Brief.

## Model discipline

No new schema; one country-level record. The constitutional-supremacy point completes the site's
review spectrum (New Zealand's parliamentary sovereignty at one pole, South Africa's constitutional
supremacy at the other), carried in prose and the tests.
