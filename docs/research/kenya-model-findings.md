# Kenya — model findings

What Kenya added to the comparative picture, and what it reused.

## 1. Devolution without devolved justice — a second decentralised-but-national case

South Africa, earlier in this batch, showed that a quasi-federal state can run justice entirely
nationally. Kenya makes the same point from the other direction: a **devolved unitary** state with 47
county governments, whose Fourth Schedule nonetheless keeps courts, criminal law, policing and
correctional services in the national sphere. Having both cases — one quasi-federal, one devolved
unitary — alongside the true sub-national federations (Mexico, Argentina, India) makes the modelling
rule legible: **a record exists where a tier does justice work, not merely where a tier exists.**
Modelled as one country record; carried in prose and the tests.

## 2. A prosecutor who can direct an investigation — a new point on the prosecution axis

Kenya's Director of Public Prosecutions is constitutionally independent (Article 157(10)), de-linked
from the Attorney-General (Article 156) — and, unusually, can **direct the Inspector-General to
investigate** an allegation of criminal conduct (Article 157(4)). Set against the police
**investigation firewall** (Article 245(4)), which bars anyone else from directing the
Inspector-General about a particular investigation, the DPP becomes the single office that can
lawfully start a specific investigation. That is a distinct point on the prosecution-independence
axis the batch has been mapping — beyond the autonomous-but-passive prosecutions (Portugal, the NPA's
middle position in South Africa) to an autonomous prosecution with an affirmative investigative
trigger. Reused schema; the fact lives in prose.

## 3. A third shape of constitutional review

Kenya has constitutional supremacy (courts can strike down inconsistent law) but **no separate
constitutional court**: the High Court holds the constitutional-review jurisdiction (Article
165(3)(d)), with appeals rising to a **2010-created apex Supreme Court** (Article 163). That is a
distinct shape alongside the site's others — the diffuse-no-strike-down model (New Zealand), the
diffuse-with-strike-down federations (Argentina, the US), the separate constitutional courts (South
Korea, Indonesia, the European records), and South Africa's apex-court-that-is-also-the-constitutional
-court. Reused with existing values and prose.

## 4. A religious-personal-status court inside the national structure

The Kadhis' courts (Article 170) sit among the subordinate courts and are limited to Muslim
personal-status matters where all parties profess Islam. Like Indonesia's Aceh arrangement and
Greece's Mount Athos, this is a special-jurisdiction feature recorded **in prose**, not as a separate
jurisdiction record — it does not run a parallel criminal-justice system.

## 5. Oversight as a distinguishing feature

Like South Africa, Kenya has an unusually **complete** oversight architecture — the Article 59
commissions (human rights, gender and equality, and the administrative-justice Ombudsman), the
Judicial Service Commission, the Independent Policing Oversight Authority, and the National Police
Service Commission. Recording that fullness is as much a comparative fact as recording an absence
elsewhere (Singapore, Argentina's vacant Ombudsman).

## Net schema change

**None.** Kenya reused the unitary/national country-level record; devolution-without-devolved-justice,
the prosecutor's investigative trigger, the diffuse-review-with-2010-apex shape, and the Kadhis'
courts all live in prose and the tests.
