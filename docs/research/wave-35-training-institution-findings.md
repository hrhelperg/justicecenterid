# Training institutions as institutions — model findings

Research findings from Knowledge Expansion Wave 35. Two guides, two new sources, two named
institutions: the Politieacademie (Netherlands) and Politihøgskolen (Norway).

## The gap

Wave 24's `what-a-police-academy-is` covers five systems and owns three things: that the word
"academy" does not travel well, that institutional training is usually not the whole of training,
and that who runs an academy changes what it is.

What it does not reach is **what such a body is constituted as in law**, or **what the balance of
its work actually is**. Neither `zbo` nor the recruit-versus-continuing split appears anywhere in
the corpus before this wave.

## Part of the police, and independent of it, at the same time

The Politieacademie states both halves in consecutive sentences:

> De Politieacademie is sinds 2017 onderdeel van de politie.
>
> Maar het is wel een zelfstandig bestuursorgaan (zbo). Dit betekent dat de Politieacademie zelf
> beleid maakt en haar taken uitvoert.

The page does not treat these as a contradiction, and they are not one. Being inside the
organisation is a fact about position; being an independent administrative body is a fact about who
decides what it does.

**The reporting line runs past the police entirely** — "De Politieacademie valt direct onder het
ministerie van Justitie en Veiligheid."

**And quality is judged from two directions.** The Inspectie Justitie en Veiligheid "controleert de
kwaliteit van onze opleidingen en examens"; the NVAO "is verantwoordelijk voor het beoordelen van de
kwaliteit van het hoger onderwijs". One asks whether the training serves justice and safety, the
other whether it is sound as higher education. An institution answerable to both cannot satisfy
either by satisfying the other.

**What is deliberately not concluded:** what the zbo form entails in Dutch administrative law. The
statute was not read, and no legal consequence is claimed from the label beyond the page's own gloss.

## Most police training is not recruit training

Politihøgskolen publishes its student distribution, and it inverts the public picture of a police
academy:

| Category                       | Students   |
| ------------------------------ | ---------- |
| Post- and continuing education | ~3,600     |
| Bachelor                       | 1,490      |
| Master's                       | 100        |
| **Total**                      | **~5,190** |

**Continuing students outnumber bachelor students by more than two to one.** The largest part of the
institution's teaching is for people who are already police officers.

That does not diminish initial training — the bachelor is three years and the corpus describes it
elsewhere. It says that a police training institution is mostly doing something the public picture
of it leaves out entirely. The word "academy" suggests a threshold you pass through on the way in;
a body where continuing students dominate is better described as the service's permanent teaching
institution.

The institution's stated mandate names three functions together — "utdanning, forskning og
formidling" — so teaching is one of three, not the whole.

**And it dates its own beginning:** origins traced to 1920, when a first cohort of 24 students
enrolled at Statens politiskole. Twenty-four students to five thousand in a century is a change in
what kind of institution it is, not only in how many attend.

**What is deliberately not concluded:** any degree-awarding or university status. The page names
bachelor and master programmes but does not describe itself as a _høgskole_ in the self-description
read, so no status claim rests on the name.

## Why this matters beyond the two institutions

If most police teaching happens after entry, then most of what a service can change about how its
officers behave is reachable long after recruitment — and so is most of what an outside body could
examine. Public attention concentrates almost entirely on selection and initial training, which the
figures here suggest is the smaller half of the story.

And where a training institution sits decides what it can teach when teaching it is unwelcome. A
body wholly inside a service can have its curriculum adjusted by the people whose conduct that
curriculum describes; a body with its own legal standing and an external accreditor has somewhere to
stand.
