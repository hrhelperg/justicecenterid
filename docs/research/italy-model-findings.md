# Italy — model findings

## What reused cleanly

Everything. Italy is unitary for justice — one `country`-level record, all five functions `own`,
`coverage: 'partial'`, sourced. No new field, level, scope, or authority basis.

## The finding: a regionalised state that is still unitary for justice — the anti-Åland

Italy is the instructive complement to Finland. Both are non-federal states with autonomous regions.
But where Finland's Åland holds legislative competence over policing (so it earned a `fi-aland`
record), **Italy's five special-autonomy regions hold NO justice competence at all**: Constitution
Art. 117(2) reserves jurisdiction, civil and criminal law, administrative justice and public order to
the State exclusively, and Art. 116(3) caps even the maximum devolution over "justice" at the
organisation of the Justice of the Peace. So Italy is modelled as a single `it` record with all
functions `own`, and **no special-autonomy-region record is created** — a region record exists only
where it does justice work, and these do not. This is the discipline working in the negative: a
politically decentralised state that is, for justice, fully centralised.

## What did NOT need a new value

- **Multiple national police forces** (Polizia di Stato, the military Carabinieri, the Guardia di
  Finanza) — institutional composition, kept in prose; `policingScope: 'own'` (Italy administers its
  own police nationally) is correct. The `polizia locale` carve-out (Art. 117(2)(h)) is a local
  administrative-police note, not a criminal-justice function.
- **Prosecutors are magistrates within the independent judiciary**, with mandatory prosecution
  (Art. 112) — an attribute of the prosecution arrangement, stated in prose. It is the strongest
  judicial-integration of the prosecution in Batches A–B; `prosecutionScope: 'own'` still holds.
- **Three top courts plus a dedicated Constitutional Court** — prose (like Belgium's three courts,
  but with a constitutional court, unlike the Nordics' diffuse review).
- **The pending "Nordio" reform** — published in the official gazette on 30 October 2025 but NOT in
  force (approved below two-thirds, so subject to a possible Art. 138 referendum). Recorded in prose,
  neutrally and by attribution, NOT as a `ScheduledChange`: its entry into force is contingent on a
  referendum whose date is not officially fixed, so it is not "enacted-with-date".
