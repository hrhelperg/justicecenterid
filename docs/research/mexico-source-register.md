## Neutrality (the load-bearing point)

The **2024 judicial reform** is stated as **enacted fact** with its gazette citation and dates
(Arts. 96, 116-III, 94; first elections 1 June 2025; new Supreme Court installed 1 September 2025;
second round 2027). Every **assessment** is carried only by attribution to the **IACHR** and the
**OAS Electoral Observation Mission**, by name and date; the site adds no view of its own. A test
scans all reader-facing Mexico prose and fails on partisan or characterising terms.

## Corrections from verification

- The Art. 21 investigation clause is **paraphrased**, not quoted from stale text (the current text
  adds the federal security secretariat and the Guardia Nacional to the list).
- The Guardia Nacional reform's amended-article list is **not enumerated** (the brief's list was
  incomplete); the fact stated is only that the GN was placed under the Secretariat of National
  Defence by a September 2024 gazette decree.

## Two currency points

- The **World Prison Brief figures updated** between the research pass (265,874 at 30 Apr 2026) and
  authoring (268,245 at 31 May 2026); the current values are used.
- The World Prison Brief's **"ministry responsible" label** names the Comisión Nacional de Seguridad,
  dissolved in 2018; it is not used.

## Model note

Mexico is a genuine dual-tier federation — all four functions replicated federally and in each of 32
entities — modelled as a **federal record + two representative State records** (Jalisco, Nuevo León),
reusing the US/India pattern. Harmonisation is high (one national procedural code, one accusatory
model, and since 2024 a uniform elected-judiciary model on the states).
