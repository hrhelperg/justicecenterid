# Wave 20 source register — public safety, emergency powers and civil protection

Branch `feat/knowledge-expansion-wave-20`. Compiled 2026-08-26.

**133 unique documents were fetched and read this session. 79 access failures were recorded.**
Both numbers matter: a register that showed only successes would be describing a different
research session from the one that happened.

## 1. The standard applied

Inherited from the France pilot and applied unchanged:

- **HTTP 200 is not verification.** Every entry below was confirmed to contain the text it is
  cited for. `verificationMethod: 'content-confirmed'` is the only value that means this.
- **HTTP 403 does not mean unavailable.** Official legal databases routinely serve readers
  normally and bot-wall automated requests. A 403 is recorded as a 403, never as an absence.
- **A blocked source is never described as read.** Section 3 lists every one.
- **URLs are never constructed from a pattern.** Only URLs actually fetched are recorded.

Tier-1 for this wave: constitutions, statutes, official legislation databases, constitutional and
supreme courts, parliaments, official gazettes, and official civil-protection or
emergency-management agencies describing their own statutory function. Tier-2 may supplement but
never silently replaces primary authority for a load-bearing legal claim. Two Tier-2 sources are
used in this wave and both are named as such below.

## 3. What the access failures actually cost

reason several matrix cells read `NOT ESTABLISHED`:

1. **`echr.coe.int`** — the Council of Europe's own Convention PDF returned 403 to both an

## 4. Adversarial verification of the sources themselves

Every graded claim was put to three independent adversarial verifiers, each with a different
lens — source authenticity, overgeneralisation, and country-scope leakage / false equivalence —
each instructed to refute rather than agree and to re-fetch the load-bearing URLs.

**313 verdicts: 278 CONFIRMED, 31 OVERSTATED, 2 REFUTED, 2 UNVERIFIABLE.**

The two refutations and the most consequential overstatements are recorded here because they
shaped the content:

- **REFUTED — ICCPR Art. 4 filed as Canada's derogation rule.** It is not. The Covenant is
  unincorporated in Canadian law and creates no domestic cause of action; the Emergencies Act
  preamble only directs the Governor in Council to _have regard to_ it. **Canada has no domestic
  derogation mechanism at all** — limitation runs through Charter s. 1 and, for a legislature,
  s. 33. This became a finding rather than a deletion.
- **REFUTED — "all requests for a US disaster declaration must be made by the Governor".** 42
  U.S.C. §5170(b)(1) gives the Chief Executive of an affected Indian tribal government a parallel
  power to request one, and §5191(b) lets the President act on an _emergency_ declaration without
  any Governor request. Corrected.
- **OVERSTATED — the US Suspension Clause described as a "derogation" provision.** Derogation is
  ECHR/ICCPR vocabulary with no counterpart in US constitutional law; the Suspension Clause
  suspends a remedy, it does not derogate from a rights obligation. Calling it one would have been
  exactly the false equivalence this wave's brief warns against.
- **OVERSTATED — New Zealand declaration rules generalised from single gazetted instances.** Both
  narrowed to what the gazetted notices actually show, with the statutory rule marked as not read.
