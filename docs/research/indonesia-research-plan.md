# Indonesia — research plan

Indonesia is country 5 of Batch C. It is unitary (low jurisdiction-model pressure) but adds two
distinctive institutional facts and a careful sub-national religious-law feature.

## Questions the pilot had to answer

1. **Federal or unitary?** — a unitary decentralised state (Pasal 1(1), Pasal 18). All four justice
   functions are national; no provincial ownership. One `id` record, all functions `own`.
2. **Where do the police sit?** — the National Police (POLRI) is a single national force that reports
   directly to the President, not to a ministry (Law No. 2 of 2002, Pasal 8). A distinctive reporting
   line.
3. **How many apex courts, and what review model?** — two: the Supreme Court (over four court
   environments) and a separate Constitutional Court (2003) with concentrated review of statutes
   (Pasal 24(2), 24C). The two divide review — the Constitutional Court on statutes, the Supreme
   Court on sub-statutory regulations.
4. **Where does prosecution sit?** — the Kejaksaan is within the executive but functionally
   independent (Law No. 11 of 2021), and is dominus litis (master of the case); the police
   investigate.
5. **Any reforms?** — yes: new national codes, the KUHP (Law 1/2023) and a new KUHAP (Law 20/2025),
   in force 2 January 2026, replacing the Dutch-colonial penal code; and a 2024 machinery-of-
   government split that moved prisons to a new Ministry of Immigration and Corrections.
6. **Aceh?** — the province applies Islamic criminal law (qanun jinayat) to Muslims through Sharia
   courts, but those courts sit within the national judiciary — a bounded special-autonomy feature,
   not a federal transfer.
7. **Prisons and numbers?** — a national service; World Prison Brief: 287,571 at 30 Jun 2026 against
   capacity 152,707 — 188.3%, severe overcrowding.

## Handling Aceh honestly

Aceh's Islamic criminal law is a genuine sub-national legal feature, but the Sharia courts remain
part of the national religious-court environment under the Supreme Court, and police/prosecution/
prisons stay national. So — following the Greece/Mount Athos precedent — it is described in prose
with attribution (Law 11/2006, Qanun 6/2014, Mahkamah Syar'iyah), not modelled as a separate
jurisdiction record. It is stated factually, without editorialising.

## Sources targeted

The 1945 Constitution, the Police and Prosecution laws, the State Secretariat (new codes), the
Ministry of Immigration and Corrections, the Aceh laws, and the World Prison Brief — the load-bearing
passages confirmed verbatim in two passes.

## Model discipline

No new schema. The distinctive facts (police under the President, dual apex, Aceh) live in prose and
in the tests; the corrections-ministry and World-Prison-Brief currency points are handled by using
the current values and naming the out-of-date label.
