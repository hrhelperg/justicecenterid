## The federalism finding (the data-model driver)

Verbatim from the Second Schedule and Chapters VI–VII: **Police** is Exclusive List item 45 and
s 214(1) bars any force other than the Nigeria Police Force → **federal**. **Prisons** is Exclusive
List item 48 → **federal** (the Nigerian Correctional Service). **Courts** — s 6(5) makes both the
federal courts (Supreme Court, Court of Appeal, Federal High Court, FCT High Court) and the state
courts (State High Courts, Sharia and Customary Courts of Appeal) superior courts of record → **both
tiers**, though appointment/discipline/funding are unified under the federal NJC and the apex is
federal. **Prosecution** — AGF for federal offences (s 174), State AG for state offences (s 211) →
**dual**. **Criminal law** — not an Exclusive-List item, so residual/state (s 4(7)); states run the
Criminal Code (south), the Penal Code (north) and Sharia penal codes (twelve northern states) →
**state**, alongside federal offences. So Nigeria is modelled as a **federal record + two illustrative
state records** (Kano, Lagos): the sub-national tier runs courts, prosecution and criminal law but
**not** police or prisons — the **inverse** of India, Mexico, Argentina and the US.

## Corrections / precision notes from verification

- **NCoS Act 2019** — substance (renaming from the Nigerian Prisons Service; a non-custodial arm)
  confirmed; the exact signing day was not pinned by a fetched source, so it is dated only to **August
  2019** / "a 2019 Act".
- **NHRC amendment** — the 1995 Act was amended (quasi-judicial powers); the amendment was **assented
  in 2011**. Stated as "a later amendment" without over-precise dating.
- **Two prison reference dates** — population (Jun 2026) vs capacity/occupancy (Jun 2025) kept
  distinct; no fresh occupancy computed by mixing them.
- **Neutrality** — the Sharia penal codes and the state-police bill are stated by dated attribution;
  the "safeguards sufficient" / "risk of abuse" debate is attributed to proponents and critics, not
  adjudicated.
