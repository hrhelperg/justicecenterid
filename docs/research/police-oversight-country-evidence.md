## Norway — Spesialenheten for politisaker

**Instrument:** påtaleinstruksen (FOR-1985-06-28-1679) chapter 34, §§ 34-1 to 34-10, in the
version in force from 15 August 2025.

- **§ 34-1.** In cases concerning whether an employee of the police or the prosecuting authority
  has committed a criminal act _in the course of duty_, the investigation "skal … foretas og
  ledes av et eget sentralt organ, Spesialenheten for politisaker". The unit is a "riksdekkende
  etterforskings- og påtalemyndighet" — a nationwide investigating **and prosecuting** authority.
- **§ 34-1.** Persons employed or holding office in the unit may not simultaneously be employed
  in the police or the prosecuting authority; those appointed to an office carry a two-year
  quarantine from any previous such employment. The Ministry of Justice holds overall
  administrative responsibility.
- **§ 34-3.** The Chief decides the prosecution question except where it belongs to the
  riksadvokaten. Where the unit brings an indictment, the case in court is conducted by the
  Chief, the assistant chief, or another member holding a law degree. **It prosecutes.**
- **§ 34-5.** Reports are of a _criminal act_ (anmeldelse). The Chief **must reject** cases
  falling outside the unit's subject-matter competence, "for eksempel fordi det anmeldte forhold
  ikke er straffbart" — for example because the reported matter is not criminal. **A conduct
  complaint that alleges no offence is not this body's business.**
- **§ 34-6 second paragraph.** Even with no suspicion of a criminal act, an investigation
  **shall** be opened where someone dies or is seriously injured as a result of the police's or
  prosecuting authority's performance of duty, or while in their care.
- **§ 34-7 second paragraph.** Where matters emerge suggesting the case should be assessed
  administratively, the Chief **shall send the case** to the relevant politimester or head of
  særorgan. **It does not discipline.**
- **§ 34-9.** The riksadvokaten may order the unit to open, carry out **and stop** an
  investigation. Independence here is real but bounded, and the bound is in the same chapter.
- **§ 34-10.** Annual report to the Ministry of Justice and the riksadvokaten.

## Czechia — Generální inspekce bezpečnostních sborů (GIBS)

**Instrument:** Act No. 341/2011 Coll., consolidated text in force 1 January 2026.

- **§ 1(1).** GIBS is established as an **ozbrojený bezpečnostní sbor** — an armed security
  force in its own right. It is not a civilian review body and not a department of a ministry.
- **§ 1(2).** The director is appointed and removed by the **prime minister**, on the
  government's proposal and after discussion in the Chamber of Deputies' security committee, and
  is answerable to the prime minister. The line does not run through the Interior Ministry.
- **§ 1(4).** GIBS is an organisational unit of the state whose income and expenditure form a
  **separate chapter of the state budget**.
- **§ 2(1)–(2).** Its task is to search out, detect and investigate facts indicating a crime
  committed by an officer of the **Police of the Czech Republic, the Customs Administration or
  the Prison Service**, by its own members, and by state employees assigned to those bodies where
  the offence relates to their work. **The remit is not police-only.**
- **§ 2(3).** It conducts reliability tests on those persons.
- **§ 2(4)–(5).** It monitors and evaluates information about unlawful activity by those persons,
  proposes preventive measures, and issues methodological recommendations to the individual forces.
- **§ 10, § 11, §§ 23–27.** It may restrict personal liberty, detain, and use coercive means and
  weapons — police powers, exercised by an inspection with police-type authority.
- **Complaints.** The Act contains no complaint procedure. It provides instead that anyone may
  **alert** (upozornit) the inspection to shortcomings in its own activity, to conduct by _its
  own_ members amounting to a criminal, administrative **or disciplinary** offence, or to conduct
  by a member of another security force amounting to a **criminal offence only**. GIBS must
  accept the alert and inform the person within 30 days. The asymmetry is in the statute: a
  non-criminal complaint about a police officer is not within it.
- **Discipline.** No disciplinary power over the forces it investigates.
- **Prosecution.** None. GIBS is a policejní orgán under the trestní řád; the státní zástupce
  decides the prosecution question, and offences by GIBS's own members are investigated by the
  státní zástupce directly.

## South Africa — Independent Police Investigative Directorate

**Instrument:** Independent Police Investigative Directorate Act 1 of 2011, Government Gazette
No. 34298, 16 May 2011.

- **§ 4(1).** "The Directorate functions independently from the South African Police Service."
  The independence stated is from the **Service**, and the Act does not extend it to the Minister.
- **§ 6.** The Minister nominates the Executive Director; the relevant parliamentary committee
  confirms or rejects within 30 parliamentary working days; the term is five years, renewable
  once; the Minister may remove on the grounds in § 6(6).
- **§ 28(1).** The Directorate **must** investigate: deaths in police custody; deaths as a result
  of police actions; any complaint relating to the discharge of an official firearm; rape by a
  police officer, on or off duty; rape of any person in police custody; any complaint of torture
  or assault by a police officer in the execution of duty; corruption matters within the police;
  and any other matter referred by the Executive Director, Minister, MEC or Secretary.
- **§ 28(2).** It **may** investigate systemic corruption involving the police.
- **§ 29(1).** A Station Commander or any member of the **South African Police Service or a
  Municipal Police Service** must notify the Directorate immediately of any § 28(1)(a)–(f)
  matter and file a written report within 24 hours. The remit covers municipal services too.
- **§ 7(4)–(5).** The Executive Director **must refer criminal offences to the National
  Prosecuting Authority** for prosecution; the NPA must notify the Directorate of an intention
  to prosecute. **IPID does not prosecute.**
- **§ 7(6)–(7), § 30.** Disciplinary matters are referred to the National or Provincial
  Commissioner, who **must within 30 days initiate disciplinary proceedings in terms of the
  recommendations** and report quarterly to the Minister. The duty to act is real; the
  disciplinary decision remains the Service's. **IPID does not discipline.**
- **§ 35(6)(b).** Service complaints about the SAPS transfer to the **SAPS** and where
  appropriate the Secretariat — not to IPID. **IPID is not the destination for every complaint
  against the police.**

## Kenya — Independent Policing Oversight Authority

**Instrument:** Independent Policing Oversight Authority Act, **Cap. 86** (originally Act No. 35
of 2011; revised by the 24th Annual Supplement, Legal Notice 221 of 2023, as at 31 December 2022).

The long title states the Act provides "for **civilian oversight** of the work of the Police".

- **§ 5.** Objectives: hold the Police accountable to the public; give effect to Article 244 of
  the Constitution; ensure independent oversight of the handling of complaints by the Service.
- **§ 6(a).** Investigate complaints of **disciplinary or criminal** offences by any member of
  the Service, on its own motion or on complaint, and make recommendations — including for
  **prosecution, compensation, internal disciplinary action** or other relief — and **make public
  the response received** to those recommendations.
- **§ 6(b).** Receive and investigate complaints **by** members of the Service.
- **§ 6(d).** Monitor, review and **audit** investigations and actions taken by the **Internal
  Affairs Unit** of the Service, and keep a record of all complaints regardless of where first
  reported.
- **§ 6(e).** **Conduct inspections of Police premises**, including detention facilities.
- **§ 6(g).** Review the patterns of police misconduct and the functioning of the internal
  disciplinary process.
- **§ 7(1)(a).** Powers to requisition records; enter premises including police premises (warrant
  required for a private home); seize; interview under oath; **summon and compel attendance**,
  including of serving or retired officers.
- **§ 7(1)(a)(ix).** **Recommend to the Director of Public Prosecutions** the prosecution of any
  person. **IPOA does not prosecute.**
- **§ 7(1)(b).** **Take over** ongoing internal investigations that are inordinately delayed or
  manifestly unreasonable.
- **§ 7(1)(d)–(e).** Require the DPP to respond to a recommendation to prosecute, and require the
  Service to respond on policy and on recommendations.
- **§ 25.** Must investigate any death or serious injury resulting from police action or caused
  by members on duty; the Police must secure and supply evidence, and failure is an offence.
- **§ 26.** May **not** investigate a matter that is the subject of proceedings before a court or
  judicial tribunal.
- **§ 29.** After investigation it may recommend prosecution to the DPP, recommend disciplinary
  action, or recommend procedural improvement. **IPOA does not discipline.**

### Kenya — Internal Affairs Unit

**Instrument:** National Police Service Act, **Cap. 84**, § 87.

- Established as a Unit within the National Police Service, headed by an assistant
  Inspector-General, reporting through that officer **directly to the Inspector-General**. It is
  **internal**.
- Functions: receive and investigate complaints against the police; promote uniform standards of
  discipline and good order; keep records of complaints and investigations.
- It **recommends** disciplinary action to the Inspector-General; the Inspector-General may in
  exceptional cases authorise the Unit to conduct disciplinary proceedings.
- **"The Unit shall not be subject to the control, direction or command of the Kenya Police, the
  Administration Police or the Directorate."** An internal unit with a statutory insulation
  clause — internal position, insulated command line.

## New Zealand — Independent Police Conduct Authority

**Instrument:** Independent Police Conduct Authority Act 1988 (Authority established 1989); Crown
Entities Act 2004; Crimes of Torture Act 1989. Sections as stated by the Authority.
legislation.govt.nz refused automated requests; nothing is cited from a text not read.

- **Status.** An Independent Crown Entity — "politically independent and accountable only to
  Parliament for our performance and use of taxpayer funding". The Authority states "we are not
  part of the NZ Police" and "under law we are fully independent".
- **§ 12.** Receive complaints alleging misconduct or neglect of duty by any member of Police;
  receive complaints concerning any Police practice, policy or procedure affecting a complainant;
  investigate incidents in which a member of Police acting in execution of duty causes or appears
  to have caused **death or serious bodily harm**.
- **§§ 17–18.** Must notify Police of any complaint received; Police must notify the Authority of
  any complaint they receive within five working days.
- **§ 17.** Where a complaint should be resolved by Police action, the Authority may recommend
  that and co-ordinate the resolution.
- **§§ 23–26.** The **same powers as a Commission of Inquiry** to summon witnesses and gather
  evidence; Police must provide all information and assistance.
- **§§ 27–28.** On completing an investigation it must determine whether any Police act or
  omission was **unlawful, unreasonable, unjustified, unfair or undesirable**, inform Police of
  its findings, and may recommend — including recommending disciplinary or criminal proceedings.
- **§ 27.** It has **no power to lay criminal charges or take disciplinary action**.
- **§ 29.** If dissatisfied with the Police response it must inform the Attorney-General and the
  Minister of Police, and the Attorney-General must inform **Parliament**.
- **Crimes of Torture Act 1989 / OPCAT.** Monitors places of Police detention.

## Denmark — Den Uafhængige Politiklagemyndighed

**Instrument:** Act no. 404 of 21 April 2010, in force 1 January 2012; Act no. 1172 of 8 June
2021, in force 1 July 2021 and 1 January 2022; provisions carried in the Administration of
Justice Act (retsplejeloven).

- **English name.** The Authority's own English pages call it **the Police Complaints
  Authority**. The Danish name carries "Uafhængige" (independent); the body's official English
  rendering does not. The Danish name is used first here for that reason.
- **Jurisdiction.** Handles **conduct complaint cases** and investigates **criminal cases**
  concerning police personnel, prosecutors, other police and prosecution personnel performing
  police or law-enforcement tasks, and defence personnel assisting the police. **Both mandates in
  one body.**
- Initiates investigations where a person has died or been seriously injured as a result of
  police intervention or while in police custody.
- "The Police Complaints Authority is independent of both the police and the prosecution
  service."
- **Governance.** Governed by the Police Complaints Council — its supreme governing body, chaired
  by a **High Court judge** — with day-to-day management by a chief executive. Around 40 staff.
  It shares premises and management with the Independent Evidence Oversight Board
  (Bevismiddeltilsynet).
- **Prosecution.** NOT ESTABLISHED from a Tier-1 source in this wave. No claim is made either way.

## Ireland — Fiosrú and the Policing and Community Safety Authority

**Instrument:** Policing, Security and Community Safety Act 2024 (No. 1 of 2024), commenced
2 April 2025.

### Fiosrú — Office of the Police Ombudsman (Part 6)

- **§ 195–199.** Complaints by members of the public; recording; time limits; admissibility.
- **§ 200–202.** Categories of complaint **suitable for resolution by An Garda Síochána**, with
  statutory arrangements for handling them. **Not every admissible complaint is investigated by
  Fiosrú.**
- **§ 203.** Matters concerning the death of, or serious harm to, a person.
- **§ 204.** Notification to the Police Ombudsman of an incident of concern.
- **§ 205, § 207.** Investigation of matters in the public interest; investigation of matters
  relating to the Garda Commissioner.
- **§ 209–211.** Powers equivalent to a member of An Garda Síochána when undertaking an
  investigation; search of Garda Síochána premises; additional investigative powers.
- **§ 214.** **Referral to the Director of Public Prosecutions.** Fiosrú does not prosecute.
- **§ 215.** Other referrals following investigation, including for disciplinary action. Fiosrú
  does not discipline.
- **§ 218.** Functions under the Coroners Acts 1962 to 2020.

### Policing and Community Safety Authority (Part 4)

- **§ 121–122.** Establishment; objective, functions and powers.
- **§ 142–151, Chapter 4 "Inspections by Authority".** **Inspectors of policing services**;
  inspections by the Authority; prescribed inspection body; joint inspections; inspection plan;
  powers of an inspector; reports of inspections undertaken on the Authority's own initiative and
  at the Minister's request; monitoring and assessment of measures taken on recommendations.
- **§ 133–134.** Duty of the Garda Commissioner to facilitate the Authority's functions; duty of
  the Director to assist it.

### Temporal

- **§ 154, Chapter 6.** **Dissolution of the Policing Authority.**
- **§ 163, Chapter 7.** **Dissolution of the Garda Síochána Inspectorate.**
- The Garda Síochána Ombudsman Commission became **Fiosrú** on 2 April 2025.

Three predecessor bodies — GSOC, the Policing Authority and the Garda Síochána Inspectorate —
are **historical** from that date. Two of them were dissolved into a single successor, which is
why "the Irish police inspectorate" no longer names a body that exists.

## Sweden — the arrangement that has no watchdog

**Instrument:** Polismyndigheten's own statement of the department's position, in Swedish.

- **Avdelningen för särskilda utredningar (SU)** is "en **oberoende avdelning inom
  Polismyndigheten**, avskild från övrig polisverksamhet" — an _independent department inside the
  Police Authority_, separated from other police activity.
- It investigates suspected crimes by, among others, police employees, police students, **judges
  and prosecutors**.
- "det är alltid en åklagare vid **Särskilda åklagarkammaren**, SÅK, som är
  förundersökningsledare i SU:s ärenden" — a prosecutor at the Special Prosecution Chamber is
  **always** the preliminary-investigation leader in SU's cases.
- **Särskilda åklagarkammaren** is a national unit **inside the Swedish Prosecution Authority**.

Sweden has no standalone external police-oversight body. The function is split between a
ring-fenced department inside the police and a specialised chamber inside the prosecution
service. Sweden is the counterexample that stops the dedicated-body taxonomy being stated as a
rule — and the phrase "oberoende avdelning **inom** Polismyndigheten" is the clearest evidence in
the wave that _independent_ and _external_ are different claims.

## Austria — a general mandate, correctly classified

**Instrument:** B-VG Art. 148a (already in the corpus from the Austria pilot, via RIS).

The Volksanwaltschaft is a constitutional body to which **anyone may complain of alleged
maladministration in federal administration**, comprising three Ombudspersons, and it serves as
Austria's National Preventive Mechanism under OPCAT with human-rights commissions.

Its mandate is **general public administration**, not policing. Police fall inside it because
they are federal administration, not because the body is a police body. Austria is already
carried on `/institutions/ombuds-and-rights-institution` and that placement is correct. Wave 7
adds no Austrian route and makes no police-specific claim about the Volksanwaltschaft.

## France — carried forward

Wave 6 established from Légifrance that the IGPN is "un service actif de la direction générale de
la police nationale"; that the director general of the gendarmerie "dispose de" the IGGN; that
the Défenseur des droits is an "autorité administrative indépendante" with a security-deontology
mandate covering security activity generally and no power to sanction; and that the CGLPL may
visit places of deprivation of liberty at any time and has no power to sanction.

France's contribution to Wave 7 is the inversion: the bodies **inside** the police hold the
investigative powers, and the bodies **outside** it do not.
