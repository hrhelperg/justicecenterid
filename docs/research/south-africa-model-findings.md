# South Africa — model findings

What South Africa added to the comparative picture, and what it reused.

## 1. The constitutional-supremacy pole — completing a spectrum

New Zealand, earlier in this batch, gave the site its first parliamentary-sovereignty system, where
the courts cannot strike down a statute. South Africa gives it the clearest opposite: **constitutional
supremacy**, where "law or conduct inconsistent with [the Constitution] is invalid" (s 2) and the
courts can and do strike down legislation. The site's review spectrum is now complete at both ends —
parliamentary sovereignty (New Zealand) ↔ diffuse review with strike-down (Argentina, the US) ↔
concentrated constitutional courts (South Korea, Indonesia, and the European records) ↔ apex-court
constitutional supremacy (South Africa). Carried in prose; no schema change.

## 2. "Quasi-federal" that is not sub-national — the honest counter-case

South Africa is the batch's demonstration that a country with provinces does **not** automatically
need sub-national records. Mexico, Argentina and India earned them because their states/provinces run
their own courts, police and prisons. South Africa's provinces run **none** of the four functions —
the schedules put courts, prosecution, prisons and criminal law in the national sphere, and give
provinces only police _oversight_ (s 206(3)). So it is modelled as **one country record**, and the
dossier says explicitly why. Having both the sub-national federations and this quasi-federal-but-
national case in the same batch makes the modelling rule legible: a record exists where a tier does
justice work, not merely where a tier exists.

## 3. An apex court that is also the constitutional court

South Africa's Constitutional Court has been the highest court for _all_ matters since the 2013
Seventeenth Amendment — a third shape of constitutional review alongside the diffuse (New Zealand
can't strike down; Argentina/US can) and the separate-constitutional-court (South Korea, Indonesia)
models. It is a single supreme court that is simultaneously the constitutional apex. Reused with
existing values and prose.

## 4. A prosecution poised between autonomy and ministerial responsibility

The NPA is a single national authority that must act "without fear, favour or prejudice" (s 179(4))
yet over which the Justice Minister holds "final responsibility" (s 179(6)) — a designed middle
position between the constitutionally autonomous prosecutions (Portugal, Mexico, Argentina) and the
minister-directed ones. It adds another distinct point to the prosecution-independence axis the batch
has been mapping.

## 5. Oversight as a distinguishing feature

Where several batch countries have thin or absent oversight (Singapore's recorded absences, Argentina's
vacant Ombudsman), South Africa has an unusually **complete** oversight architecture — Chapter 9
institutions, a Judicial Service Commission, an independent police-investigation directorate, and a
judicial inspectorate for prisons. Recording that fullness is as much a comparative fact as recording
an absence elsewhere.

## Net schema change

**None.** South Africa reused the unitary/national country-level record; the constitutional-supremacy
pole, the quasi-federal-but-national modelling, and the rich oversight all live in prose and the
tests.
