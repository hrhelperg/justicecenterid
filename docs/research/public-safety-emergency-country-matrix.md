# Wave 20 country matrix — emergency powers, civil protection and public safety

Branch `feat/knowledge-expansion-wave-20`. Compiled 2026-08-26 from primary sources fetched and
read this session. Research infrastructure: **publication follows evidence, not the other way
round.** Several systems below appear in no published page because their evidence did not reach
the standard, and several dimensions are `NOT ESTABLISHED` for every system researched.

Vocabulary, used strictly:

- **ESTABLISHED** — a Tier-1 source was fetched, read, and quoted, and it states the proposition.
- **PARTIALLY ESTABLISHED** — the source implies it, or an official Tier-2 source states it, or
  the text was read only as reproduced inside another official document.
- **NOT ESTABLISHED** — it could not be confirmed from a source actually read.

**`NOT ESTABLISHED` never means "no".** A blank cell records a gap in this platform's sourcing.

## 2. Who may declare, and who must agree

The single most jurisdiction-specific dimension in the wave. No two systems answer it alike.

| System        | Declared by                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Legislature's role                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Czechia       | Three tiers, three declarants: **the Government** (_nouzový stav_); **Parliament on a government proposal** (_stav ohrožení státu_, absolute majority of all deputies and all senators); **Parliament** (_válečný stav_)                                                       | Extension of _nouzový stav_ beyond 30 days requires the **prior** consent of the Chamber of Deputies                                                  |
| Spain         | Three regimes, three declarants: **Government by decree** (_alarma_); **Government after prior authorisation of the Congress** (_excepción_); **the Congress itself**, by absolute majority (_sitio_)                                                                          | Extension of _alarma_ beyond 15 days only with express congressional authorisation, and the Congress may set the terms of the extension               |
| Germany       | _Verteidigungsfall_ determined by the **Bundestag** with Bundesrat consent, two-thirds of votes cast and at least a majority of members; by the **Joint Committee** if the Bundestag cannot convene; **deemed made** if federal organs cannot act while an attack is under way | The determination _is_ the parliamentary act                                                                                                          |
| Ireland       | The Art. 28.3.3 shield is switched on by **each House of the Oireachtas** resolving that a national emergency exists                                                                                                                                                           | Constitutive, and also required to end it                                                                                                             |
| Sweden        | Activation of the _krigsdelegation_ is **not a governmental act** — it is decided by the members of the Advisory Council on Foreign Affairs after consulting the Prime Minister where possible                                                                                 | The War Delegation _becomes_ the Riksdag                                                                                                              |
| Brazil        | **President alone**, after non-binding consultation of two councils                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Congress decides on the _estado de defesa_ within ten days by absolute majority; the _estado de sítio_ requires **prior** congressional authorisation |
| South Africa  | **President** by proclamation in the Gazette — under the **Act**, not the Constitution, which does not name the declaring authority                                                                                                                                            | Extension by the National Assembly, first by majority, subsequent extensions by at least **60 per cent**                                              |
| Kenya         | **President**, expressly subject to Art. 58                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Extension by resolution after **public debate**: first extension two-thirds, subsequent extensions three-quarters of all members                      |
| France        | Decree in the Council of Ministers; beyond **twelve days** extension requires a **statute** fixing the duration                                                                                                                                                                | The extension statute is the control                                                                                                                  |
| Australia     | **Governor-General**, on the satisfaction of three "authorising Ministers" (call out) or of the Prime Minister (national emergency declaration)                                                                                                                                | Not constitutive                                                                                                                                      |
| Canada        | **Governor in Council** on reasonable grounds                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Confirmation motion; **either House** negativing it revokes the declaration                                                                           |
| United States | **President**, proclamation transmitted to Congress and published in the Federal Register                                                                                                                                                                                      | Termination by joint resolution; **continuation needs no legislative approval**                                                                       |
| New Zealand   | The responsible **Minister** (national); a person appointed under s. 25 CDEMA (local)                                                                                                                                                                                          | Not established from the Act itself — legislation.govt.nz was 403                                                                                     |
| Japan         | **Prime Minister** after cabinet deliberation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Diet approval sought within twenty days; Diet may resolve disapproval or vote to repeal                                                               |

## 3. Duration and renewal

| System                   | Initial maximum                                                                       | Renewal                                                    |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| New Zealand              | **7 days**                                                                            | By the same Minister; extensions gazetted                  |
| France                   | **12 days**                                                                           | Only by statute                                            |
| Kenya                    | **14 days**                                                                           | 2 months at a time; two-thirds then three-quarters         |
| Spain (_alarma_)         | **15 days**                                                                           | Only with express congressional authorisation              |
| South Africa             | **21 days**                                                                           | 3 months at a time; majority then ≥60 per cent             |
| Brazil (_defesa_)        | **30 days**                                                                           | **Once only**, for an equal period — a hard 60-day ceiling |
| Czechia (_nouzový stav_) | **30 days**                                                                           | Only with prior consent of the Chamber of Deputies         |
| Spain (_excepción_)      | **30 days**                                                                           | Once, for an equal period, on the same requirements        |
| Canada                   | **30 / 60 / 90 / 120 days** by class of emergency                                     | Continuation under the Act                                 |
| Australia (NED)          | **3 months**                                                                          | Repeatable, each extension capped at 3 months              |
| Switzerland              | Security ordinance lapses at **6 months** unless a draft statutory basis is submitted | One extension, then a further 6 months                     |
| United States            | **1 year**, terminating on the anniversary                                            | By **unilateral presidential continuation notice**         |
| Germany                  | No fixed term; ends when its preconditions disappear                                  | —                                                          |
| Ireland                  | **No fixed term**, and the shield does not lapse when the conflict ends               | Ends only when both Houses resolve it has ceased           |

Two findings this table exists to carry. First, the range is **7 days to no fixed term** — an order
of magnitude and then some, so "temporary" carries no common content across systems. Second, the
United States is the outlier on renewal: it is the only system researched in which continuation
requires no act by anyone other than the person who declared it.

## 4. What may be restricted, and what may never be

Five distinct architectures for the same problem. The differences are not stylistic.

| Architecture                            | Systems                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Mechanism                                                   |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A list of non-derogable rights**      | ICCPR Art. 4(2) — seven articles; ECHR Art. 15(2) — four, with an express carve-out on Art. 2 for deaths from lawful acts of war; South Africa's Table of Non-Derogable Rights; Kenya Art. 25 — four rights that "shall not be limited"                                                                                                                       | Enumerate what is out of reach                              |
| **A core-content guarantee**            | Switzerland BV Art. 36(4) _"Der Kerngehalt der Grundrechte ist unantastbar"_; Germany GG Art. 19(2) _Wesensgehaltsgarantie_ with the Art. 79(3) eternity clause                                                                                                                                                                                               | Protect a core of every right rather than list whole rights |
| **A closed list of permitted measures** | Brazil Art. 139 — under _estado de sítio_ on the Art. 137(I) ground, **only** seven enumerated measures may be taken against persons; Art. 136 §1 for _estado de defesa_                                                                                                                                                                                      | Invert the question: nothing not listed is available        |
| **A closed list of suspendable rights** | Spain Art. 55(1) — a fixed list, and only under _excepción_ or _sitio_, **never** _alarma_                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Invert differently: only what is listed may be reached      |
| **No derogation architecture at all**   | Japan (no emergency chapter, no derogation clause — the "public welfare" qualifier inside each right does the work); Czechia (the Listina has no derogation clause); Canada (no domestic derogation mechanism — Charter s. 1 limitation and s. 33); Norway (no written derogation clause; two constitutional amendment proposals pending before the Storting) | Ordinary limitation doctrine carries the whole load         |

South Africa adds two absolute bars that belong in no other column: no emergency legislation may
**indemnify the state or any person for an unlawful act**, and none may derogate from s. 37 itself.

## 5. Who may review an emergency act — and whether the declaration is reviewable at all

| System       | Forum                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | What may be reviewed                                                                          |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| South Africa | **Any competent court**                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | The declaration, any extension, and any legislation or action taken in consequence (s. 37(3)) |
| Kenya        | **The Supreme Court** specifically (Art. 58(5))                                                                                                                                                                                              | The same three things — but the forum is narrowed to one court                                |
| Spain        | **Only the Tribunal Constitucional** for the declaration, because the decree has _rango o valor de ley_ (STC 83/2016); the ordinary courts for administrative acts under it (LO 4/1981 Art. 3)                                               | Split by the rank of the instrument                                                           |
| Czechia      | The Constitutional Court held in **Pl. ÚS 8/20** that a government resolution declaring a _nouzový stav_ is primarily an _akt vládnutí_ which in principle **is not subject to review**                                                      | The counter-example                                                                           |
| France       | Art. 16 carries a built-in clock: after **thirty days** of exceptional powers the Conseil constitutionnel may be seised; emergency measures get full proportionality review by the administrative judge; _référé-liberté_ under CJA L. 521-2 | Multiple routes, different objects                                                            |
| Switzerland  | BV Art. 189(4): acts of the Federal Assembly and Federal Council **cannot as a rule be challenged** before the Federal Supreme Court; BV Art. 190 makes federal statutes binding on courts                                                   | Structurally constrained                                                                      |
| Germany      | GG Art. 115g: even in the _Verteidigungsfall_ the constitutional position and functions of the **Bundesverfassungsgericht** may not be impaired                                                                                              | Protected by name                                                                             |
| New Zealand  | Ordinary courts on ordinary administrative-law and Bill of Rights grounds — _Borrowdale v Director-General of Health_ [2020] NZHC 2090 found nine days of an unlawful limitation                                                             | Ordinary supervision, not relaxed                                                             |
| Japan        | Supreme Court's general constitutional review under Arts. 81 and 98, with **no emergency carve-out**                                                                                                                                         | Everything                                                                                    |

Judicial review of emergency powers is therefore **not universal in identical form**: one system
gives it to any court, one to the apex court only, one splits it by the rank of the instrument, one
holds the declaration itself in principle unreviewable, and one constrains review of federal acts
structurally.

## 7. Military assistance to civil authorities

The dimension with the highest false-equivalence risk, so the cells are stated narrowly.

| System        | The boundary, in the instrument's own terms                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| United States | **18 U.S.C. §1385** criminalises willful use of the armed forces to execute the laws except where expressly authorised by the Constitution or an Act of Congress; the Insurrection Act supplies the exception, and §251 is State-request-gated                                                                                                                                                        |
| Germany       | GG Art. 87a(2): armed forces may be deployed for non-defence purposes **only insofar as the Basic Law expressly permits**; an Art. 87a(4) internal deployment must be discontinued if **either** chamber demands                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Norway        | Grunnloven § 25(3): the Government has **no right** to use military force against the inhabitants except pursuant to statute, save where an assembly disturbs the public peace and does not immediately disperse                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Australia     | ADF must assist and cooperate with State/Territory police; must not be used for a particular task unless a police member of that jurisdiction requests it; **must not stop or restrict protest, dissent, assembly or industrial action** except where death, serious injury or serious property damage is reasonably likely. Disaster assistance under s. 123AA confers **no coercive powers at all** |
| Japan         | SDF disaster dispatch is **request-driven** from a prefectural governor; SDF personnel may exercise a mayor's restricted-area power **only where the mayor or anyone able to exercise it is not present**                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Switzerland   | The army **supports** civil authorities; deployment is subsidiary                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Spain         | The UME is **requested** by the Interior Minister and **ordered** by the Defence Minister                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Kenya         | The KDF **shall** assist in emergencies, and must **report to the National Assembly** whenever deployed                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| South Africa  | **Only the President** may authorise employment of the defence force, for three purposes only, and must then inform Parliament                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Sweden        | Support involving force against persons is confined by statute to **counter-terrorism**, on cumulative conditions                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Ireland       | The counter-example: Art. 40.4.5 **disapplies the habeas corpus section** as against acts of the Defence Forces during war or armed rebellion                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |

## 8. Whether an emergency reallocates competence

| Shifts competence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Preserves it                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Germany GG Art. 115c(1): in the _Verteidigungsfall_ the Federation acquires concurrent legislative power over Länder subject matters                                                                                                                | **Norway's _ansvarsprinsippet_** — the body responsible for a field in normal times also bears responsibility for preparedness and for handling extraordinary events. No shift by default                                                                     |
| Sweden RF 15:3: the War Delegation exercises the Riksdag's powers, subject to an express carve-out                                                                                                                                                  | **EU TFEU Art. 6(f)**: civil protection is a _supporting_ competence — the Union may only support, coordinate or supplement, and Decision 1313/2013 Art. 1(3) expressly preserves Member States' primary responsibility                                       |
| Spain LO 4/1981 Art. 9: under _alarma_, civil authorities and Autonomous Community and local police in the affected territory come under the direct orders of the competent authority                                                               | **Canada** Emergencies Act s. 3: nothing is a national emergency if it can be effectively dealt with under any other law of Canada — and s. 14(2) bars a federal declaration confined to one province unless that province's cabinet indicates it cannot cope |
| Australia NED Act s. 15: a Minister may vary, disapply or substitute Commonwealth law — but only from a **closed list of administrative formalities**, and s. 15(8) walls off criminal procedure, intelligence, surveillance and integrity statutes | **Brazil** CF Art. 60 §1: the Constitution may **not be amended** while federal intervention, _estado de defesa_ or _estado de sítio_ is in force                                                                                                             |
|                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | **Spain** Art. 116(5): the Congress may not be dissolved while any of the three states is in force, and the functioning of the constitutional powers may not be interrupted                                                                                   |

## 9. What this matrix does not establish

- No cell records how often any mechanism has been used, how long it took, or whether it worked.
- No system is ranked and no "strongest" or "most protective" judgment is made or implied.
- No casualty, response-time, detention-total or effectiveness figure was researched or recorded.
- Fifty-three of 256 cells are `NOT ESTABLISHED`. Each is a gap in this platform's sourcing.
- The four supranational instruments bind states party to them. **None is evidence of any
  particular state's domestic law**, and no national cell in this document rests on one.
