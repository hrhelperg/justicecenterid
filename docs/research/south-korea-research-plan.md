# South Korea — research plan

South Korea is country 2 of Batch C. It is unitary (low model pressure on the jurisdiction schema)
but carries two things of real value: a dual-apex court structure with concentrated review, and a
prosecution in active, politically charged restructuring — the batch's first genuine `ScheduledChange`.

## Questions the pilot had to answer

1. **Federal or unitary?** — unitary (Constitution Art. 3; local self-government limited to local
   matters, Arts. 117–118). One `kr` record, all functions `own`; the 2021 autonomous-police layer is
   administrative decentralisation within the single national force, noted in prose, not a record.
2. **How many apex courts, and what review model?** — two: the Supreme Court (14 Justices) and a
   separate Constitutional Court (9 adjudicators). Review is **concentrated** — a court refers a
   constitutionality question to the Constitutional Court (Art. 107).
3. **Where does the prosecution sit, and is it changing?** — under the executive (Ministry of
   Justice), headed by the Prosecutor General. A 2020–2021 reform moved most investigation to the
   police; an **enacted** 2025 Government Organization Act amendment will abolish the Prosecution
   Service on 1 October 2026 and split investigation (to a Ministry of the Interior and Safety
   agency) from indictment (to a Ministry of Justice office).
4. **Who investigates?** — since 2021 the police (National Investigation Headquarters) lead most
   investigations; prosecutors keep the warrant request, a supplementary role and indictment. The
   2025 restructuring will formalise the split.
5. **Prisons and numbers?** — the Korea Correctional Service under the Ministry of Justice. World
   Prison Brief: 65,279 at 29 Jan 2026 against capacity 50,614 — 129.0%, above capacity (same date,
   so a valid occupancy claim).

## Neutrality discipline (contested reform)

The prosecution restructuring is politically charged. Enacted facts are stated with dates and
attribution (the Government Organization Act amendment; Korea Herald reporting); the pro-reform
rationale and the constitutionality controversy are attributed to their sources, not stated in the
site's voice; and enacted-but-not-yet-effective is kept distinct from in force. The current
arrangement is stated as remaining in force until 2 October 2026.

## Sources targeted

The 1987 Constitution (Constitute), the U.S. Federal Judicial Center's Korea profile, the Supreme
Prosecutors' Office site, Korea Herald (for the 2025 restructuring), and the World Prison Brief.
Secondary reading covers the police ministry, the 2020–2021 rebalance, and the human-rights/anti-
corruption bodies — all flagged.

## Model discipline

No new jurisdiction schema. The one genuine model exercise is the **scheduled change**: the enacted
restructuring meets the certainty gate (`enacted-with-date`, fixed commencement), so it is recorded
as a `pending` `ScheduledChange` rather than only in prose — the first such record since the
foundation pilots.
