# Indonesia — model findings

What Indonesia added to the comparative picture, and what it reused.

## 1. A police force that reports to the head of state

Indonesia adds a police reporting line no other country on the site has: the National Police (POLRI)
"is under the President" and its Chief is responsible directly to the President (Law No. 2 of 2002,
Pasal 8), not to a Minister of the Interior, Home Affairs or Justice. Across the site, the police sit
under a ministry; Indonesia routes them to the head of state. This is a country-level attribute
carried in prose (`policingScope: 'own'`), and it makes the "which ministry runs the police"
comparison genuinely varied.

## 2. Aceh — a sub-national religious-criminal-law feature handled like Mount Athos

Aceh applies Islamic criminal law (qanun jinayat) to Muslims through Sharia courts (Mahkamah
Syar'iyah). This is a real sub-national criminal-law variation — but the Sharia courts sit within the
national religious-court environment under the Supreme Court, and police, prosecution and prisons
stay national. So Aceh is a **bounded special-autonomy feature**, not a federal transfer, and it is
described in prose with attribution — the same treatment given to Greece's Mount Athos, and the
opposite of India's genuine State-level ownership (which earned sub-national records). Two Batch C
countries now show the honest handling of a sub-national feature that does _not_ rise to a modelled
jurisdiction.

## 3. Dual apex with a divided review

Indonesia reuses the dual-apex shape (Supreme Court + Constitutional Court) already seen for South
Korea and several European records — but with a clean division: the Constitutional Court reviews
statutes against the Constitution, the Supreme Court reviews sub-statutory regulations against
statutes. Carried in prose; no schema change.

## 4. Currency discipline paid off twice

Indonesia surfaced two currency traps that the batch's rules caught:

- **The prison figure had updated** between the research pass and authoring (276,136 at 31 Dec 2025 →
  287,571 at 30 Jun 2026). Because the prison figure is re-confirmed by hand for every country, the
  current value was used — the stale research number would have been wrong.
- **The World Prison Brief's ministry label is out of date** ("Ministry of Justice and Human Rights"),
  because Indonesia split off a new Ministry of Immigration and Corrections in 2024. The dossier uses
  the current ministry and explicitly notes the source's stale label rather than repeating it.

Both are instances of the same principle the batch runs on: report the current value from the source,
and name where the source itself is behind.

## 5. Prosecution: executive-branch but functionally independent

Indonesia's Kejaksaan is "within the executive branch … but independent, free from the influence of
governmental power" — a distinct point on the prosecution spectrum from the fully-under-a-minister
and the constitutionally-autonomous models, and it is stated in the enabling law's own words.

## Net schema change

**None.** Indonesia reused the unitary country-level record and the dual-apex prose pattern; the
police-under-the-President fact, the Aceh feature, and the new codes live in prose and in the tests.
