## The federalism finding (the data-model driver)

Verbatim from the **Fourth Schedule Part 1** (national government): item **7** — "Police services,
including— (a) the setting of standards of recruitment, training of police and use of police
services; (b) **criminal law**; and (c) **correctional services**"; item **8** — "**Courts**".
Prosecution is a national constitutional office (Art. 157), so it is not listed among the shared
functions. **Part 2 (county governments)** — read in full — gives the 47 counties agriculture, county
health, transport, trade, planning, pre-primary education, firefighting and the like, and **none** of
courts, police, prisons or prosecution. So despite devolution, all four functions are national —
modelled as **one country record, no sub-national record** (like South Africa; the opposite of
Mexico, Argentina and India, which got sub-national records).

## Precision notes applied from verification

- **Prison-stat provenance.** The World Prison Brief attributes the **prison-population total
  (60,740)** to the national statistics bureau and the **general-population base (59.98 m, mid-2025)**
  to **UN figures** — so the rate of 101 derives from the UN base, not the statistics bureau. Stated
  that way, not conflated.
- **Two reference dates.** The population is a **2025 average**; the capacity (34,000) and occupancy
  (~176.5%) are **December 2023**. The dossier states the source's dated figures faithfully and does
  **not** compute a fresh occupancy by mixing them.
- **Article 245(4)** also bars directions to the Inspector-General on the employment/promotion/
  discipline of members (para (c)); the dossier's (a)/(b) investigation-and-enforcement point is not
  presented as the whole of 245(4).

## Deliberate omissions

Current office-holders are not named (they change, and one reported Commissioner-General surname
spelling could not be resolved against an official source). The exact statutory Act numbers behind
**IPOA** and the **Article 59 commissions** were not re-fetched line by line. Reform/current-events
matters — the **2022 BBI litigation**, the **2023 police-reform taskforce**, proposals still before
Parliament, and the **2024 protests** and their policing — are left out as contested, unresolved or
non-structural.
