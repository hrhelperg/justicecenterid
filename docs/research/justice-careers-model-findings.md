# How the justice professions are entered — model findings

Research findings from Knowledge Expansion Wave 28. Five guides, ten official sources, two systems:
France and England and Wales.

This wave answers a question the corpus had recorded against itself in three separate places and
deferred each time.

## The gap this closes

Three records said, in their own words, that qualification routes were unresearched:

- the `judge` profession record carries a `trainingRouteShape` that is structural and names no
  country;
- the `defence-lawyer` record said "qualification routes were not researched for any country … so
  trainingRouteShape is structural only and names no country";
- `/defence/who-may-act-as-a-lawyer` says in its own scope callout that it "does not describe
  qualification routes".

The boundary that follows is clean and is enforced by tests: **a profession record describes the
shape of a route without naming a country; these guides describe what named systems publish.** A
guide that stopped naming systems would be restating the record above it, and a record that started
naming systems would be duplicating the guide below it. Both directions now fail a test.

## What the research found

### There are two models, and they answer different questions

|              | Career judiciary (France)             | Appointment from practice (England and Wales)        |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Entry        | Competitive examination               | Selection by a commission                            |
| Prior career | Not assumed                           | Five to seven years of post-qualification experience |
| Training     | 31 months as an _auditeur de justice_ | None: the practice was the training                  |
| Timing       | Early                                 | After a career is established                        |

One asks _can this person be trained to judge?_ The other asks _has this person already
demonstrated it?_ Neither is the lesser form, and the durations show why: 31 months of dedicated
training against five to seven years of practice. Both are long; they spend the time in different
places.

The clearest tell is age. France's mid-career route requires a candidate to be "âgé de moins de 57
ans au 1er janvier de l'année du concours" — an upper limit. England and Wales sets no upper limit
and instead requires years of prior practice, which is a lower one. Neither system states a
philosophy of judging; both state a threshold, and the thresholds point in opposite directions.

### The two French durations are a trap

31 months belongs to the initial route. **12 months** belongs to the separate mid-career route, which
sits on top of years of prior professional practice. Presenting either as "the training a French
judge receives" is wrong about the other. A mutation proof merged them and initially survived.

### One corps or two is settled by one sentence

Article 1 of the Ordonnance n° 58-1270 constitutes the _corps judiciaire_ as comprising the
magistrats du siège **and** du parquet, together with the auditeurs de justice, and provides:

> Tout magistrat a vocation à être nommé, au cours de sa carrière, à des fonctions du siège et du
> parquet.

That is the whole architecture in one line. It does not say the functions cooperate or that movement
is permitted; it says every magistrat has a vocation to serve in both. Entry is to the corps, and
the division into bench and prosecution is a division of function inside it.

England and Wales has no such structure. Judicial office is reached by appointment; a Crown
Prosecutor is an **employed lawyer**, and the service's own page describes annual leave, a
contributory pension and an internal training programme. There is no shared entrance because there
is no shared body to enter.

The structure does not settle independence — it relocates the question. One arrangement must show
that the judging half is independent of the prosecuting half; the other must show that an employed
prosecutor is independent of an employer.

### You do not need a law degree to become a solicitor

The regulator states the degree "can be in any subject or an equivalent level 6 qualification", with
central assessments in its place. This is the finding most easily destroyed by a careless
paraphrase: it would be natural to write "usually a law degree", and the regulator does not say
that. The source note records the prohibition explicitly, and a test enforces it.

What replaces the law degree is a central examination — which relocates the guarantee from the
university to the assessment.

### Every route ends with a supervised stage, and it is the one nobody removes

| Arrangement                | As published                                                                                     |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Pupillage                  | "a non-practising period, usually of six months, and a practising period, usually of six months" |
| Qualifying work experience | "two years' full time or equivalent"                                                             |
| Auditeur de justice        | 31 months                                                                                        |

An examination can establish that a candidate knows a rule. It cannot establish that they will
notice which rule matters on a real matter where the cost of error falls on someone else. The
mechanism is not observation but **responsibility**: somebody already qualified answers for the work.

The durations tell you what preceded them — the supervised stage is longest exactly where the least
came before it. And it is the stage that limits how many can qualify, because an examination can be
sat by anyone who registers while a supervised place requires a supervisor willing to be
responsible. How places are allocated was not researched, and that is the part most often asked
about.

### A published absence is a finding

The prison service of England and Wales publishes **no academic qualification requirement**. It
assesses a scenario-based test and a values-based assessment _before_ any interview, plus medical
and fitness testing.

That absence is reported as an absence — never as a claim that qualifications are irrelevant, and
never as a claim that none is applied at any later stage. The same discipline applies to the
prosecution source, which states no qualification threshold at all and is therefore cited only for
the employment relationship.

A route requiring a degree, central assessments and two years of supervised work, and a route
requiring none of them, staff the same justice system. Neither is the serious one.

## What is deliberately not here

**No provider, course, tutoring service or coaching is named anywhere in this wave**, and no route
is recommended. **No published fee, salary, trainee wage or pupillage award is reproduced**, though
every body cited publishes them and quoting them would be trivially accurate.

This boundary is sharper than in any previous wave for a specific reason: legal qualification is
surrounded by a large commercial preparation market in a way police recruitment is not. Every page
in this cluster sits one sentence away from becoming a lead magnet for it. The rule is therefore
absolute rather than balanced.
