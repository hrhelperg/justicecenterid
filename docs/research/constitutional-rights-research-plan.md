# Wave 21 research plan — constitutional rights, state powers and fundamental safeguards

Written after the baseline (`knowledge-expansion-wave-21-baseline.md`), before any page was
drafted. The evidence gathered against it is in `constitutional-rights-matrix.md`; the
publication decisions are in `constitutional-rights-cannibalization-matrix.md`.

## 1. The problem this wave actually faces

Wave 21's nominal subject — rights, state power, limits — is the corpus's densest ground. Twenty
of the fifty-eight research domains in the brief are already owned by pages that answer their
reader's question well. The wave therefore cannot be a rights catalogue, and the first research
question is not "what are the rights?" but **"what does the corpus not yet know?"**

The baseline answered that with three findings:

1. The corpus has the **emergency exception without the ordinary rule**. It explains derogation,
   non-derogable rights and five emergency architectures, and never explains how a qualified right
   is lawfully limited on an ordinary Tuesday.
2. The corpus explains **investigation as institutional competence, never as a rights problem**.
   `seizure` and `freedom of expression` appear zero times; `privacy` twice, both incidental.
3. The corpus has never asked **who a constitutional right belongs to**, or **who it binds**.

## 2. Method

**Function first, terminology second, route naming third** (Part B). Every domain was researched
by asking what legal work the provision does, then recording the word each system uses for it,
then deciding whether the corpus needed a page.

**Primary text only, read directly.** Every constitutional provision quoted in this wave was
extracted from the official publisher's own consolidated text and read in context. Where a
translation exists alongside an authoritative original, both were pulled and compared — the
German _Deutschengrundrechte_ finding, on which a whole page rests, was confirmed against
`gesetze-im-internet.de`'s German text, not against its English translation, because the finding
is about a word.

**Verified negatives count as evidence.** Four of this wave's load-bearing findings are absences
established by exhaustive search of a primary text, not by silence:

| Search                                                                                   | Result | Where it is used          |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ | ------------------------- |
| `Selbstbelastung`, `schweigen`, `Aussage zu verweigern`, `Geständnis` in the Grundgesetz | 0      | the silence page          |
| `incriminat`, `silent`, `silence`, `confession` in the GG English text                   | 0      | the silence page          |
| `equality of arms` in the ECHR text                                                      | 0      | the equality-of-arms page |
| `proportion*` in the ECHR text; `proportion*` in the ICCPR                               | 0, 0   | the proportionality page  |

An absence in a text is a fact about that text. It is not a fact about that legal system, and no
page in this wave treats it as one — Germany's _nemo tenetur_ principle exists, and this wave says
only that the Basic Law does not contain it, which is what was actually verified.

## 3. Domains researched, and what happened to each

Part D lists fifty-eight domains. They were sorted by what the corpus already owns.

**Owned; not re-researched, not republished (28 domains).** Rule of law, legality,
non-retroactivity, legal certainty, equality before the law, access to justice, effective remedy,
due process, procedural fairness, presumption of innocence, unlawful evidence, detention review,
habeas corpus, constitutional review, judicial review, appeal, judicial independence, interpreter
and court language, counsel, legal aid, case-file access, arrest and detention safeguards, use of
force, prosecutorial discretion, derogation, non-derogable rights, emergency limitation,
temporariness.

**Researched and published (12 routes).** Limitation architecture; proportionality and necessity;
rights-holder scope; who fundamental rights bind; domestic effect of international instruments;
the clock before a judge; privacy as a protected interest; entering and searching a home;
intercepting communications; silence and self-incrimination; equality of arms; public hearings.

**Researched and deferred (5 domains), with the reason recorded rather than the gap hidden.**

| Domain                         | Why deferred                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Digital evidence, surveillance | Saying anything specific needs per-jurisdiction statutory research not done here, and this is the wave's highest safety-risk area (Part K). Publishing a thin page here would be worse than publishing none.                   |
| Bodily samples, DNA safeguards | Constitutional text reaches only bodily integrity (ZA s. 12(2), CZ Art. 7(1)). The sampling regimes are statutory and were not researched.                                                                                     |
| Police discretion              | `why-police-accountability-matters` owns the structural argument and `prosecutorial-discretion-and-legality` owns the legality/opportunity distinction. A Wave 21 page would need police-act research not done here.           |
| Impartiality of the tribunal   | `courts-during-a-state-of-emergency` already carries GG Art. 101, CF Art. 5º XXXVII and CE Art. 117(5)–(6). What is left — impartiality as distinct from independence — rests on treaty recitals already quoted in four pages. |
| Assembly, expression, movement | Reached only as _examples_ of holder-scoped and qualified rights. A cluster of its own would require political-neutrality work (Part U) this wave did not do.                                                                  |

**Rejected outright (3).** A general "rights vs state power" page (owned by
`limits-on-public-power`); any new emergency-rights page (owned by Wave 20 and
`which-rights-can-never-be-suspended`); any US-framed "search and seizure" page (Part B).

## 4. Jurisdictions

Fourteen were named in the brief. Twelve carried verifiable primary text for this wave's
questions and are used: Germany, Switzerland, South Africa, Kenya, Canada, Brazil, Spain, Japan,
Netherlands, Ireland, Czechia, United States. France and Australia were **not** used for
substantive Wave 21 claims — see the source register for why.

Supranational: ECHR and ICCPR, both read in full text. Neither is ever used to establish a
national position; the corpus invariant forbidding that is re-proved this wave.

## 5. The editorial spine

Part I's question, carried by every page in the wave:

> Who may exercise what power, under what legal authority, for what purpose, subject to what
> limits, and with what review?

The limitation page states it structurally. The investigation pages state it for one measure at a
time. The rights-holder and addressee pages state it as a scope question. No page asserts that
state power is presumptively illegitimate, and no page asserts that a right is a zone the state
may never enter.
