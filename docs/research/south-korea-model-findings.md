# South Korea — model findings

What South Korea added to the comparative picture, and what it reused.

## 1. The batch's first genuine ScheduledChange — an enacted, dated, pending restructuring

South Korea is the first country since the foundation pilots to earn a `ScheduledChange` record. The
distinction from Batch B matters: Italy's separation-of-careers bill and Austria's
Bundesstaatsanwaltschaft draft were **proposals** (certainty below `enacted-with-date`), so they were
kept in prose and deliberately not put in the scheduled-change registry. Korea's restructuring is an
**enacted** amendment to the Government Organization Act with a **fixed commencement** (Supreme
Prosecutors' Office abolished 1 October 2026; successor agencies from 2 October 2026), which meets
the certainty gate. So `kr-prosecution-restructuring-2026` sits as `pending` / `enacted-with-date`,
and the current arrangement is stated as in force until the effective date. This exercises the model
exactly as designed — and it is the first **non-European** scheduled change.

## 2. A prosecution mapped across time, not just in space

Batch B mapped where prosecutions sit on a spectrum. Korea adds a **time axis**: the same function is
described in three states — historically prosecutor-directed investigation and near-exclusive
indictment; the current post-2020–2021 arrangement (police lead investigation, prosecutors keep the
warrant request and indictment); and the enacted 2026 split (investigation under the interior
ministry, indictment under the justice ministry). The "who directs the investigation" comparison is
therefore explicitly date-dependent here, which the scheduled change makes legible.

## 3. Dual apex + concentrated review — reuse of an existing shape

Two apex courts (Supreme Court + a separate Constitutional Court) with **concentrated** review (a
court refers a constitutionality question to the Constitutional Court, Art. 107) is the same family
as several European records already published — the opposite of New Zealand's diffuse,
no-strike-down model earlier in this batch. Carried in prose; no schema change.

## 4. Neutrality on a charged reform — the Poland playbook, reused

The prosecution restructuring is politically contested. The pages state enacted facts with dates and
attribute both the pro-reform rationale and the constitutionality controversy to their dated sources,
adding no view in the site's voice — the same discipline used for Poland in Batch B, now applied to a
non-European reform and paired with a structured scheduled change.

## 5. World Prison Brief, over-capacity variant

Korea's World Prison Brief figures carry the population and the capacity at the **same** reference
date (29 Jan 2026), so — unlike New Zealand's stale-capacity case earlier in the batch — a current
occupancy is validly stated: 129.0%, above capacity. Same source, opposite handling, both honest.

## Net schema change

**None** to the jurisdiction model. The genuine model exercise is the scheduled change, using the
existing `ScheduledChange` shape for the first time in the batch.
