# Italy — research plan

## Jurisdiction scoping

Regionalised unitary ("Regional State"), not federal; 20 regions, 5 with special autonomy (Art. 116).
KEY QUESTION: do the special-autonomy regions hold any justice competence? ANSWER: no — Art. 117(2)
reserves jurisdiction, criminal law, public order and prisons to the State exclusively; the most a
region may gain over "justice" is the organisation of the Justice of the Peace. So justice is fully
national — one `it` record, all `own`; no region record. (The contrast to Finland's Åland.)

## Research questions

- Constitutional framework (1948): the independent judiciary (Art. 104), the CSM.
- Prosecution: magistrate-prosecutors within the judiciary; mandatory prosecution (Art. 112).
- Courts: ordinary (Cassation), administrative (Council of State), and a dedicated Constitutional Court.
- Police: multiple national forces (Polizia di Stato, Carabinieri, Guardia di Finanza); the local carve-out.
- Corrections (DAP, Ministry of Justice).
- Oversight (CSM; National Guarantor).
- The pending "Nordio" separation-of-careers reform (neutral, attributed).

## Model stress test

Reuse, no new schema. Even a regionalised state with five special-autonomy regions keeps justice
fully national — the opposite of Finland's Åland. Multiple police forces and magistrate-prosecutors
are prose. See italy-model-findings.md.

## Source hierarchy

Constitution (Constitutional Court official English PDF) + Law 121/1981 (official government PDF) →
the Court of Cassation, Interior and Justice ministries, and the EU e-Justice Portal → the official
gazette (2025 reform) → Council of Europe SPACE I (prison figures).
