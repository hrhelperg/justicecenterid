# Czechia — model findings

What Czechia added to the comparative picture, and what it reused.

## 1. The most executive-attached prosecution on the site — the far end of a spectrum

Batch B has now mapped a full spectrum of prosecution independence, and Czechia anchors one end:

| country     | where the prosecution sits                                                 |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Portugal    | constitutionally autonomous (neither judiciary nor executive)              |
| Italy       | inside the independent judiciary (magistrate-prosecutors)                  |
| Austria     | a separate service, subordinate to the Minister of Justice                 |
| **Czechia** | **"a component of the executive power … part of the Ministry of Justice"** |

Czechia is the clearest "inside the executive" case: not merely under a minister but a part of the
ministry itself. The dossier states this in the service's own words and does not soften it — and it
records, with dates and attribution, the enacted 2024 reform that added tenure protections for the
Prosecutor General. Reporting both the executive attachment and the counter-reform, side by side, is
the honest way to describe a live governance question. No schema change: `prosecutionScope: 'own'`
plus prose carries it.

## 2. Tri-apex courts — a second instance of the Austria shape

Czechia has three courts of last resort: the Supreme Court, a separate Supreme Administrative Court,
and a separate Constitutional Court. This is the same structural family as Austria (OGH/VwGH/VfGH),
and it confirms the model tolerates multiple parallel apex courts without modification. Recorded as
fact; no fifth court invented.

## 3. The "no judicial council" case — recording an absence honestly

Many European justice systems have a national self-governing judicial council; Czechia does not.
Court administration is exercised by the Ministry of Justice, with only advisory councils inside
individual courts. The temptation in a templated system is to populate a "judicial council" field
because peers have one — so the dossier does the opposite: it states the absence explicitly, on both
the justice-system and oversight pages, and a test asserts that the pages say there is no national
judicial council. This is the governance-side equivalent of an honest deferral: name what is _not_
there rather than imply a peer institution exists.

## 4. Restricted claim: just under capacity

Czechia's SPACE I density is 96.4 — slightly under capacity, like Portugal (96.3) and Greece (94.7),
and the mirror image of Italy's over-capacity 118.1. Stated with the same three limitations
(single-day snapshot, national aggregate, no cross-country comparison). The high imprisonment rate
that sits behind that density (179.5 per 100,000) is deliberately kept out of the claim, because a
rate invites exactly the cross-country comparison SPACE I warns against.

## Net schema change

**None.** Czechia reused unitary/national values, the multi-apex prose pattern, and the
enacted-reform-with-dates discipline; its distinctiveness lives in prose and in the tests.
