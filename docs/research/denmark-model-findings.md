# Denmark — model findings

## What reused cleanly

Everything. Denmark is a unitary state — one `country`-level record with all five functions
`own`, identical in shape to Ireland and the Netherlands. `coverage: 'partial'`, sourced. No new
field, level, scope, or authority basis.

## What needed a new field or value

Nothing. Two structural features that a naïve model might try to encode were kept in prose:

1. **Integrated police and prosecution.** The Police Commissioner (politidirektør) is at once the
   head of the police district and the local prosecutor, and both the police and the Prosecution
   Service sit under the Ministry of Justice. This is institutional composition — how the offices
   are arranged — not a new jurisdiction level or scope. `policingScope: 'own'` and
   `prosecutionScope: 'own'` remain correct (Denmark administers both nationally); the integration
   is prose. This mirrors the United States rule that institutional composition is prose, not typed
   records.
2. **One court system for everything.** There is no constitutional court and no separate
   administrative-court branch; the ordinary courts decide those questions too (Constitution §63).
   Again an attribute of the court system, stated in prose.

## What stayed in prose

Institution names (Rigspolitiet, Anklagemyndigheden, Højesteret, Kriminalforsorgen); the
integrated police-prosecution; the prosecution's subordination to the Ministry of Justice (versus
the constitutional independence of the courts, §62); the Faroese/Greenlandic self-government; and
the honest gaps (ombudsman section number, ministerial-instruction scope).

## Restricted claim

One, in corrections: SPACE I 2024 (4,129 inmates / 4,397 capacity / density 93.9 on 31 January 2024) — below capacity, scoped as a single-day national aggregate supporting no comparison.
