# United States research plan

## Objective

Test the jurisdiction model against the most decentralised system in the programme. Publishing
the US was the means; finding where the model asserts a falsehood was the goal. Results:
[united-states-model-findings.md](./united-states-model-findings.md).

## Why the US is the strongest decentralisation test

France is unitary; Germany is federal but symmetric (sixteen Länder administering federal law).
The US adds: sub-national units holding _reserved_ rather than delegated powers; thousands of
separately governed law-enforcement agencies; offices filled by _local election_; and more than
one _kind_ of sovereign — states, a federal district under congressional plenary authority, and
tribal nations with inherent sovereignty.

## Scope decision

Thirteen candidate modules; eight published, five deferred. The gate applied per module: can
every claim be traced to a source actually read, AND can it be stated without asserting a
national uniformity the decentralised reality does not have?

## Source hierarchy applied

Tier 1 throughout: the Constitution, the United States Code, the federal judiciary, the U.S.
Marshals Service, the Bureau of Indian Affairs, the Council of the District of Columbia, and
four Bureau of Justice Statistics publications. No journalism, no secondary commentary.

The one deviation: where justice.gov and fbi.gov bot-walled automated access, the enacted
statutory text was read from the United States Code as reproduced by the Cornell Legal
Information Institute. The enacted text is authoritative; the register records where it was read.

## Sample selection

Five jurisdictions, each chosen for what it tests.

| Jurisdiction                      | Tests                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Federal (`us`)                    | A federation whose units hold reserved powers; every function `shared`                                                                                                                                       |
| California (`us-ca`)              | A state that legislates AND administers its own justice system — state courts `exclusive-subnational`, the sharp contrast with a German Land's `concurrent`; home of elected sheriffs and district attorneys |
| Louisiana (`us-la`)               | County-equivalent is the _parish_ — "not every state has counties", handled without a schema change                                                                                                          |
| District of Columbia (`us-dc`)    | A non-state under congressional plenary authority — `federal-plenary`, neither reserved-power state nor sovereign                                                                                            |
| Tribal jurisdiction (`us-tribal`) | Inherent sovereignty — the reason `authorityBasis` and the `tribal` level exist                                                                                                                              |

No record for any county, municipality or specific named tribe. The 17,541 general-purpose
state and local agencies are the reason a per-unit model is not attempted; the sample tests the
model without pretending to enumerate the country. No public state, county or tribal pages are
built.

## What was researched but not published

| Module             | Blocker                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Forensics          | No source on the federal/state/county/municipal forensic-laboratory structure; a general-knowledge description would assert a uniformity that does not exist, in a safety-sensitive section.                                       |
| Border and customs | The federal border, customs, immigration and coast functions sit across several agencies whose statutory roles were not researched.                                                                                                |
| Oversight          | Oversight is as fragmented as the institutions it watches — federal inspectors general, state and local mechanisms, city-specific civilian review, and elections themselves. No single national model exists; none was researched. |
| History            | Inseparable from slavery, Reconstruction, segregation and the treatment of Native nations. Requires archival and scholarly sources; a national summary would erase what matters.                                                   |
| Timeline           | Only three dated facts established (1791, 1953, 1973). Not a timeline.                                                                                                                                                             |

## Rules held throughout

- No institution forced into another country's terminology. "Germany's FBI"-style analogies
  are avoided in the other direction too: the FBI is described by its statutory basis, not as a
  model for others.
- Both analogies the brief names are addressed as _criticised_ shorthand, not repeated: "the FBI
  is in charge" and "the US police" are each stated as wrong, with the reason.
- No claim that federal agencies command state or local police, that every sheriff runs a jail,
  that every state has counties, that every prosecutor is a "district attorney", or that tribal
  institutions are subordinate local agencies.
- Abbreviations expanded at first use: BJS, BOP, PL 280.
- No operational content: no deployment, tactics, surveillance, or exploitation of jurisdictional
  seams.
- No legal advice; no crime sensationalism; no ranking of the US against any other country.
