# France research plan

## Objective

Test the content model against one real jurisdiction in depth. Publishing France was the
means; finding where the model breaks was the goal. Findings are in
[france-model-findings.md](./france-model-findings.md).

## Scope decision

Twelve modules were in scope. Six were published and six were not. The gate was a single
question applied per module: **can every institutional claim on this page be traced to a source
we have actually read?**

That gate is what makes the pilot honest. A twelve-module France assembled partly from general
knowledge would have looked like a more successful pilot and would have been worth less than
nothing — it would have taught us that the model permits unsourced content, which we already
knew.

## Source hierarchy applied

Tier 1 only, in practice: the Constitution, three code provisions on Légifrance, and two
Ministry of Justice pages. No Tier 2 or Tier 3 source was needed for what was published, and no
journalism was used at all.

## Method

1. Constitutional framework first, from the Conseil constitutionnel's consolidated text —
   because the territorial and judicial-authority questions constrain everything below them.
2. Statutory provisions for institutional legal status, read on Légifrance at article level
   rather than through summaries.
3. Ministry of Justice pages for structural description, treated explicitly as official
   self-description rather than neutral fact.
4. Every URL opened and read. Nothing cited from a search-result snippet alone.

## What was researched but not published

| Area                             | Status                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Forensic institutions            | No primary source read. Not published.                                                                                        |
| Prison administration, probation | No primary source read. Also blocked on restricted-claim sourcing for population, staffing and conditions. Not published.     |
| Border, customs, maritime        | Division of competence not established. Not published.                                                                        |
| Oversight bodies                 | Candidates identified (CSM, CGLPL, Défenseur des droits, inspectorates); legal bases and powers not confirmed. Not published. |
| Institutional history            | Requires archival and scholarly sources not consulted. Not published.                                                         |
| Timeline                         | Only two dated facts established; not a timeline. Not published.                                                              |

## Rules held throughout

- No institutional name translated into a foreign near-equivalent. _Parquet_, _préfet_,
  _police judiciaire_ and _magistrature debout_ appear in French with an explanation, because
  the English substitutes carry the wrong institutional baggage.
- No claim about territorial allocation between police nationale and gendarmerie, because no
  source read supports one.
- No operational content: no deployment, tactics, surveillance capability, checkpoint
  procedure, or investigative technique.
- No legal advice, and no page tells a reader what to do in a situation.
