# Singapore — source register

Research date: **2026-07-27**. Every source below was read or retrieved on that date, confirmed to
contain the facts attributed to it, and independently re-checked in an adversarial verification
pass. Articles 35, 94 and 98 of the Constitution and the World Prison Brief figures were re-confirmed
by hand (Art. 35(8) re-confirmed at authoring time). Singapore's statutes and courts operate in
English, so no translation is involved.

## Access notes and one correction

- **Singapore Statutes Online** was intermittently in maintenance during research; the load-bearing
  articles were retrieved and the key ones re-confirmed by hand.
- The **Singapore Prison Service** and **Ministry of Home Affairs** organisation pages returned 404,
  so the prison administering body is taken from the World Prison Brief.
- **Correction from verification:** an over-generalised "90% of all cases are heard in the State
  Courts" figure (traced to a secondary source, not the Judiciary) was replaced with the Judiciary's
  own wording — the State Courts handle more than 80% of the overall caseload and more than 90% of
  the total criminal caseload.
- **World Prison Brief capacity is from 2013**, so the corrections page states population + rate only
  and makes **no** current occupancy/density claim.

## Deliberate gaps

The **sitting Attorney-General's name** and the substance of the **2024 constitutional amendment**
are not asserted (no source fetched). The oversight **absences** — no general ombudsman, no
Paris-Principles national human-rights institution, no independent police-complaints commission —
are recorded as researched negatives; a contested 2026 UPR back-and-forth over establishing an NHRI
was excluded because the Government's formal response could not be independently confirmed.
