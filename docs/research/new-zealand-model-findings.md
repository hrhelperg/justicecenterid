# New Zealand — model findings

What New Zealand added to the comparative picture, and what it reused.

## 1. The first uncodified-constitution / parliamentary-sovereignty case

Every prior country on the site has a written constitution that is (in most) supreme law. New
Zealand is the first with an **uncodified** constitution and **parliamentary sovereignty**: the
courts cannot strike down an Act of Parliament, and the strongest remedy against a rights-inconsistent
statute is a **declaration of inconsistency** under the NZ Bill of Rights Act 1990, which leaves the
statute standing. This completes a spectrum the site now spans:

- **Constitutional supremacy** — a court can invalidate statutes (e.g. the constitutional-court and
  diffuse-review systems already published; South Africa, later in this batch, is the strongest form).
- **Parliamentary sovereignty** — no court can (New Zealand).

No new schema: the distinction lives in the courts-page prose. But it is a genuinely new _type_ of
system for the comparison the site exists to make.

## 2. A common-law Law-Officer prosecution — a third prosecution shape

Batch B mapped the civil-law prosecution spectrum (autonomous → inside the judiciary → under a
minister → inside a ministry → the minister _is_ the prosecutor-general). New Zealand adds the
**common-law Law-Officer** shape: no standing Director of Public Prosecutions, but the Attorney-General
and Solicitor-General superintending, private-practice **Crown Solicitors** conducting serious cases,
and the **police** prosecuting lesser matters. Independence here is a matter of constitutional
convention, not of a statute placing the office outside the executive. Carried by `prosecutionScope:
'own'` plus prose.

## 3. Police-led, not prosecutor-led investigation — the common-law counterpart

New Zealand is the site's clean example of the **common-law police-led investigation**: the police
investigate and lay charges, operationally independent of ministers (Policing Act 2008 s 16(2)), and
the prosecuting Law Officers do **not** direct the investigation. This is the exact opposite of the
magistrate- or prosecutor-directed pre-trial phase in the civil-law systems, and it makes the
"who directs a criminal investigation" comparison — one of the site's stated comparison candidates —
concrete.

## 4. A recognised indigenous source of law

Tikanga Māori, recognised in Ellis v R [2022] NZSC 114 as "the first law of Aotearoa New Zealand", is
a dimension no other record carries. It is stated strictly by attribution to the judgment, and as a
_source of law within the common law_, not as a separate court system — so it needs no schema, only
careful prose.

## 5. Honest prison numbers with a stale-capacity gap

New Zealand's World Prison Brief page carries a 2026 population but only a 2019 capacity. Rather than
divide across reference dates or repeat the source's stale occupancy percentage as if current, the
restricted claim states population + rate and makes **no** occupancy/density claim — the same
incomplete-source honesty used for Austria in Batch B, now applied to the World Prison Brief (the
non-European prison-statistics source for the whole batch).

## Net schema change

**None.** New Zealand reused the unitary/national country-level record; all four novelties
(uncodified constitution, Law-Officer prosecution, police-led investigation, tikanga) live in prose
and in the tests.
