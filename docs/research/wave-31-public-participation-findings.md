## 2. Austria: the finding Wave 30 left open, half closed

Wave 30 recorded that the Austria dossier cited `at-bvg` for Article 91 while that
record's note itemises Art. 10, 78a, 78b, 87, 90a, 92, 129/133, 144 and 148a — not
Art. 91.

**RIS is still down.** Retried this wave at three addresses, all HTTP 503:

- `GeltendeFassung.wxe?...Gesetzesnummer=10000138`
- `Dokumente/Bundesnormen/NOR12018917/NOR12018917.html`
- `eli/bgbl/1930/1/A91/NOR12002479`

So **the text of Article 91 remains unread**, and nothing here pretends otherwise.

What changed is that the Federal Ministry of Justice publishes an explanatory page that
was reachable and was read directly. It states, in its own words, which article provides
for lay participation:

> Dies sieht die österreichische Bundesverfassung (Bundes-Verfassungsgesetz) in
> Artikel 91 vor.

That is **attestation by a Tier 1 body, not the article**. The distinction is recorded in
the source note and guarded by a test: if a later edit upgrades attestation into citation,
or adds Art. 91 to the `at-bvg` note without the article having been read, the suite fails.

## 3. Jury size: two numbers published, one hole preserved

Wave 30 could publish no jury size and guarded against publishing any. This wave can
publish two, because two sources state a number for a named proceeding in a named system.

**Published:**

- **England and Wales, county court: a complete jury of eight.** Juries Act 1974 s.17,
  official revised text: a verdict may be accepted where "in a case where there is a
  complete jury of eight in a county court, seven of them agree on the verdict".
- **Austria, Geschworenengericht: eight Geschworene** with three professional judges.

**Not published, deliberately: the Crown Court complement.** Section 17 states majority
thresholds for reduced panels — ten agreeing of "not less than eleven jurors", nine of
"ten jurors" — and **nowhere states the full number**. Twelve is what a reader expects and
what nothing read here supports. The hole Wave 30 left is still a hole, it is now
labelled NOT ESTABLISHED in the jury term's jurisdiction note, and three separate guards
keep it open.

## 4. Ireland: a constitutional jury right with three carve-outs

Article 38 read verbatim in the electronic Irish Statute Book. Art. 38.5:

> Save in the case of the trial of offences under section 2, section 3 or section 4 of
> this Article no person shall be tried on any criminal charge without a jury.

The three exceptions are minor offences tried summarily (38.2), special courts where the
ordinary courts are found inadequate (38.3.1°), and military tribunals (38.4).

This extended the existing `ie-constitution` record rather than creating a new one. The
first attempt added a separate record at the same URL and the Wave 21 invariant caught it:
sources are extended, not duplicated.

## 5. The fact that breaks the tidy story

Austrian **Geschworene are a separate body and still share the sentencing decision**. The
neat model — jury decides guilt separately, judges sentence; lay judges do everything
jointly — does not survive contact with Austria. The jury term states the split, and a
guard checks every part of the page that mentions the Geschworene carries it, after a
mutation proved a page-wide search could not see one part diverging from another.
