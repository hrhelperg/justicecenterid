# Norway — model findings

## What reused cleanly

Everything. Norway is a unitary state — one `country`-level record, all five functions `own`,
identical in shape to Ireland, the Netherlands and Denmark. `coverage: 'partial'`, sourced. No new
field, level, scope, or authority basis.

## What needed a new field or value

Nothing. Two structural facts are prose, not schema:

1. **A strongly independent prosecution.** Only the King in Council — not the Minister of Justice —
   may give the prosecuting authority general instructions. This is an _attribute_ of the
   prosecution arrangement (how independent it is, and by what mechanism), not a new scope or level;
   `prosecutionScope: 'own'` remains correct. It contrasts with Denmark (prosecution under the
   Ministry of Justice) and the Netherlands (under the minister's political responsibility), all
   representable with the same scope value and different prose.
2. **Prosecutor-led, police-integrated investigation.** The lowest tier of the prosecution sits
   inside the police (police lawyers with prosecutorial powers). This is functional integration —
   institutional composition — not a territorial or federal feature; prose, per the United States
   rule. Like Denmark's integration, but with the prosecution independent rather than ministry-led.

## What stayed in prose

Institution names (Politiet, Påtalemyndigheten, Høyesterett, Kriminalomsorgen, Riksadvokaten); the
King-in-Council independence mechanism; the prosecutor-led police-integrated investigation;
constitutional review by the ordinary courts (Art. 89, no constitutional court); the non-EU status;
and the 2016 proximity-police reform (27 → 12 districts), which is a completed reform recorded in
prose (not a pending scheduled change).

## Restricted claim

One, in corrections: SPACE I 2024 (3,004 inmates / 3,616 capacity / density 83.1 on 31 January 2024) — the lowest density in the batch, below capacity, scoped as a single-day national aggregate.
