# Wave 7 — oversight taxonomy findings

Ten candidate families were put to the forcing test. **One is published.** Two are merged into
pages that already exist, three are deferred, three fail on recurrence, and one is rejected as an
invented label.

The test each candidate had to pass:

1. Do at least two jurisdictions have a body of this kind, established from a Tier-1 source?
2. Do those bodies share a **function**, not just a name?
3. Would a reader who learned this family from one country be right about the others?
4. Is it distinguishable from a page JusticeCenterID already publishes?

---

## 1. Independent police investigative body — **SUPPORTED. PUBLISH.**

Six jurisdictions have an external body with a statutory mandate to investigate **crimes**
committed by police officers: Norway, Czechia, South Africa, Kenya, Denmark and Ireland. Two of
them — Norway and Czechia — hold that mandate and **no conduct-complaint mandate at all**, which
is what proves the family is not simply a re-description of the complaints body.

The decisive evidence is negative and statutory:

- Norway, påtaleinstruksen § 34-5: the Chief **must reject** cases outside the unit's competence,
  "for eksempel fordi det anmeldte forhold ikke er straffbart".
- Czechia, Act No. 341/2011: no complaint procedure for other forces. Members of the public may
  alert the inspection to conduct by another force's officer only where it amounts to a
  **criminal offence**.

A body that must turn away a complaint disclosing no offence is not a complaints body, however
independent it is. Wave 5 currently lists both Norway and Czechia as country examples of
`independent-police-complaints-body`. That placement is a false equivalence, and publishing this
page is what corrects it.

**Why it is not a merge.** The existing page is organised around complaint intake and its
triage. This one is organised around the criminal threshold: what triggers the body, what
standard applies, who prosecutes at the end. Denmark and Ireland hold both mandates and appear on
both pages — the sets overlap, and the page says so rather than implying they are disjoint.

**Route:** `/institutions/independent-police-investigative-body`.

---

## 2. Police conduct authority — **MERGE INTO EXISTING.**

The function is real and recurs: New Zealand, Denmark and Ireland all have an external body that
receives conduct complaints, investigates, and recommends. The **name** does not recur. New
Zealand's is a Conduct Authority, Denmark's a Complaints Authority, Ireland's an Ombudsman.

Publishing a global family under New Zealand's title would promote one country's label to a
global type on the strength of English, which is the specific error this wave was built to avoid.
The function already has a page: `independent-police-complaints-body`.

**Decision: MERGE.** No new route. New Zealand's § 27 limitation and Denmark's dual mandate are
added to the existing page instead.

---

## 3. Police inspectorate — **FALSE EQUIVALENCE. DEFER.** *(Wave 6's open question, now answered)*

Wave 6 asked whether the internal and external forms are one type or two, and made the answer the
condition for reopening the page. The answer from Wave 7 evidence is **two**, and they are not
close.

| | Internal form | External form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instances | IGPN, IGGN (FR) | PCSA Chapter 4 (IE); IPOA § 6(e) (KE) |
| Belongs to | The force's own director general | Parliament / statute |
| Investigates individuals | Yes — including criminal investigation | No |
| Inspects the organisation | Yes | Yes |
| Can be directed by the force | Yes | No |

The only column they share is "inspects the organisation". On every other axis they are
opposites. A single page called *police inspectorate* would have to state that an inspectorate
both is and is not part of the police, both does and does not investigate officers — which is not
a description of an institution family but of a word.

There is also not yet a second **external inspectorate body**. Ireland's PCSA is an authority
whose functions include inspection; Kenya's is an inspection power held by a complaints body.
Neither is a body whose type is "inspectorate".

**DEFER**, with Wave 6's question now closed: the internal and external forms are two types, and
neither yet has two sourced instances of its own.

---

## 4. Internal affairs — **DEFER.**

Wave 7 found something Wave 6 did not: a genuine, statutory, non-American **Internal Affairs
Unit**, established by § 87 of Kenya's National Police Service Act (Cap. 84), with its own
functions, its own reporting line to the Inspector-General, and a statutory clause insulating it
from the operational commands.

That is one jurisdiction. The functionally equivalent bodies elsewhere are not called this and
should not be renamed to fit: Sweden's is an *Avdelningen för särskilda utredningar*, France's are
*inspections générales*. Calling all three "internal affairs" would be the same error as calling
Denmark's authority a conduct authority.

So the position has moved without the verdict changing. Wave 6 deferred this because no sourced
instance existed. Wave 7 defers it because **exactly one** does, under a label the other
instances do not share.

**What the evidence did earn** is a guide about the internal/external distinction itself, where
the three bodies can appear under their own names — which is published in this wave.

---

## 5. Professional standards unit — **INSUFFICIENT RECURRENCE. DEFER.**

Unchanged from Wave 6. No researched jurisdiction establishes a body of this name. France sets
deontology standards by code binding both forces rather than by a unit; Kenya's Internal Affairs
Unit carries the "uniform standards of discipline" function without the name.

---

## 6. Police complaints commissioner — **INSUFFICIENT RECURRENCE. DEFER.**

No researched jurisdiction has a body of this name. The office-holder form (a single named
commissioner rather than a board or authority) was not established anywhere in the set.

---

## 7. Civilian police review board — **INSUFFICIENT RECURRENCE. DEFER.**

No instance in the researched set. The bodies that are civilian in composition — Kenya, New
Zealand, Ireland, South Africa — are investigative authorities, not review boards, and they
investigate rather than review the police's own investigations. Kenya's § 6(d) *audit* of the
Internal Affairs Unit is the closest thing found, and it is a function of an investigative
authority.

---

## 8. Police ombudsman — **MERGE INTO EXISTING.**

Ireland's Fiosrú is the Office of the Police Ombudsman, and it is the only instance established in
this wave. The ombuds *form* is already covered by `ombuds-and-rights-institution`, and the
police-specific complaint function by `independent-police-complaints-body`. A third page between
them would divide the same material three ways.

**Decision: MERGE.** Fiosrú stays on the complaints-body page.

---

## 9. Police integrity commission — **REJECT.**

No researched jurisdiction has a body of this name, and the brief's warning was well aimed: the
phrase is convenient for taxonomy precisely because it is vague enough to cover anything.
Applying it as a *translation* of a body whose own name means something else would manufacture an
institution. Nothing in this wave is rendered with this label.

---

## 10. External police oversight authority — **REJECT AS A ROUTE.**

The category is real but it is a *position*, not an institution family. Its members are the
complaints bodies, the investigative bodies, the ombuds institutions, the prosecutors and the
courts — four of which already have pages. A page under this name would be a container whose
contents are all published elsewhere, and its only distinctive claim — that being external does
not make a body independent — is a conceptual point that belongs in a guide.

**Decision: REJECT as a route; the concept is published as
`/law-enforcement/internal-vs-external-police-oversight`.**

## Where a future wave should look

To reopen **external police inspectorate** as a type, a wave needs at least two bodies whose
*type* is inspection — not two bodies that hold an inspection power among others. Ireland's PCSA
is one. Scotland's HMICS and England and Wales's HMICFRS were not researched here and are the
obvious next candidates; neither is named as evidence until read.

To reopen **internal affairs**, a wave needs a second jurisdiction whose statute uses that name
for the body. Renaming Sweden's or France's units to reach two is the failure this page exists to
prevent.
