# Police recruitment — commercial opportunity map (INTERNAL)

**Internal research. Nothing here is published, and a test asserts it** — monetisation vocabulary
appears in no published guide, no country module and no source note.

Wave 25 adds **no monetisation code, no affiliate link, no paid listing, no lead form, no
application form, no vacancy and no partnership**. This maps future compatibility only.

## The rule that governs everything

**Commercial opportunity must never determine a factual conclusion.** This wave's sharpest test of
that: high-intent recruitment traffic is the most commercially valuable audience the platform has,
and the pages that would capture the most of it are exactly the ones refused — "how to pass police
vetting", "easiest country to join", salary comparisons, live vacancies. Each was rejected on
editorial grounds, and each rejection is enforced by a test and proved by a mutation.

### Why the INAPPROPRIATE cells are inappropriate

- **Citizenship/nationality/residency — sponsorship and affiliate.** The readers are disproportionately
  migrants and people with complex status. Any paid placement there — immigration services above all
  — attaches a commercial interest to the most consequential and most exploitable question on the
  platform.
- **Selection structure — fitness prep and affiliate.** Attaching preparation products to the page
  describing selection stages converts an explanation into a funnel, and creates the incentive to
  say more about assessments than the platform is willing to say.

## Where the genuine opportunity is

1. **Language preparation, on the Dutch and Irish pages.** The Netherlands requires Dutch at B1 and
   Ireland requires Irish or English. That is a specific, verifiable, non-sensitive requirement with
   an obvious legitimate product, and it does not touch eligibility judgement.
2. **Recruitment discovery, once a maintained data layer exists.** The campaign-specific page is
   the natural host: its whole subject is that requirements belong to rounds. It must not happen
   before Part AB's real-time architecture exists.
3. **University and training-provider discovery, on the entry-requirements page.** Same reasoning as
   Wave 24: it must be comprehensive rather than paid-inclusion, or it becomes the ranking the
   platform refuses to publish.

tracking volatile campaign state needs a real product, and anything touching assessment performance
needs a safety review before it needs a business case.**

## Standing rules, carried forward from Wave 24 and extended

1. Paid placement is labelled, always and visibly.
2. Sponsorship never alters a factual ranking or conclusion.
3. An affiliate relationship never determines an editorial conclusion.
4. Official sources remain authoritative for recruitment requirements regardless of any commercial
   relationship.
5. A commercial partner cannot purchase a factual claim.
6. No education provider can buy "best academy" status; no such status exists.
7. Product monetisation stays structurally separate from institutional facts.
8. **New in Wave 25:** no commercial relationship may exist with a recruiting authority whose
   requirements the platform describes, and no page may imply affiliation with one. Every country
   page carries an explicit disclaimer, and a test asserts it.
