# Portugal — research plan

Portugal is country 2 of Batch B (Central & Southern Europe). It is authored to the same discipline
as every prior pilot: research from authoritative primary sources, verify each load-bearing fact,
add only evidence-driven schema use, and defer honestly where the sources thin out.

## Questions the pilot had to answer

1. **Is Portugal federal or unitary, and do its autonomous regions touch justice?** — Constitution
   Art. 6: unitary, with the Azores and Madeira as autonomous regions. Reserved competences
   (Arts. 164(1)(u), 165(1)(c)+(p)) and the exclusion of these matters from regional legislation
   (Art. 227(1)(b)) mean the regions hold **no** justice competence. → one country-level `pt`
   record, all functions `own`, **no** region record. Same result as Italy; opposite of Åland.
2. **What is the status of the prosecution?** — Constitution Art. 219: the _Ministério Público_ has
   "its own statute and autonomy". It is neither part of the judiciary nor under a minister — a
   two-directional autonomy. This is the country's most distinctive feature.
3. **How many top courts, and is there constitutional review?** — Art. 209 lists the Supreme Court
   of Justice, the Supreme Administrative Court and the Court of Auditors; Arts. 221–222 add a
   dedicated Constitutional Court; Art. 204 gives **every** court diffuse review. Four top courts +
   diffuse review.
4. **Who polices and who investigates?** — LOIC (Law 49/2008): three national forces (PJ, GNR,
   PSP), with the most serious crimes reserved to the PJ. The prosecution directs the _inquérito_
   (CPP Art. 263); an investigating judge conducts the optional _instrução_ (CPP Art. 17).
5. **Who runs the prisons, and what can be said about numbers?** — the DGRSP, one national service
   under the Ministry of Justice. SPACE I 2024: 12,193 / 12,663 / density 96.3 (just under
   capacity), as a properly limited restricted claim.

## What was deliberately left out

Forensics and border/customs (safety-sensitive, not researched to standard — and border/customs
was reorganised when the former SEF was restructured), and institutional history and a timeline
(need scholarly and per-milestone sources). All four are deferred with reasons and produce no
routes.

## Anti-conflation guard

Portugal is **not** Brazil. A scope callout states this on the hub, and the tests assert it. No
Brazilian institution, statute or figure appears on any Portugal page.
