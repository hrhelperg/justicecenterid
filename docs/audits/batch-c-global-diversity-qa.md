# Batch C — Global diversity: QA and summary

The third authored country batch on the scaling framework, and the widest: ten countries across
five continents — New Zealand, South Korea, Singapore, India, Indonesia, Mexico, Argentina, South
Africa, Kenya and Nigeria. Branch `feat/batch-c-global-ten`, based on the Batch B tip.

## Verdict: ready

Ten countries published, each through the publication gate, verified from primary sources and an
independent adversarial fact-check pass. **No new schema; no fabrication.** Batch C's contribution is
both structural and comparative: it delivered the site's full constitutional-review spectrum, every
federal/decentralised shape (including two that were previously untested), the first genuinely
pending `ScheduledChange`, and the strictest application yet of the neutrality controls on contested
subjects.

## Model findings — no new schema, every shape now covered

Batch C added **no new field, level, or scope value.** Its findings:

1. **The constitutional-review spectrum is now complete, end to end.** Parliamentary sovereignty
   (New Zealand — courts cannot strike down a statute) ↔ diffuse review with strike-down (Argentina;
   Kenya's High Court under a 2010 apex Supreme Court; Nigeria) ↔ concentrated constitutional courts
   (South Korea, Indonesia) ↔ apex constitutional supremacy (South Africa). Every point carried by
   existing values and prose.
2. **Every federal/decentralised shape is now modelled.** Genuine sub-national justice via a `federal`
   record + representative sub-national records (India, Mexico, Argentina); the **decentralised-but-
   justice-national counter-cases** where the sub-national tier runs none of the four functions →
   one country record (South Africa's provinces, Kenya's counties); and the **inverse federation**
   (Nigeria — federal police and prisons, but state courts, prosecution and criminal law), whose two
   state records mirror India's scopes with policing/corrections inverted to `none`.
3. **The prosecution-independence axis extended past Batch B.** Kenya's constitutionally independent
   DPP that can **direct** the Inspector-General to investigate (a prosecutor with an affirmative
   investigative trigger); Nigeria's **executive-embedded** dual prosecution (AGF a Cabinet Minister,
   mirrored by 36 State AGs).
4. **The first genuinely pending `ScheduledChange`.** South Korea's prosecution restructuring is
   `enacted-with-date` and `pending` (the certainty gate's only admissible pending kind), taking the
   count from 3 to **4**. Every other Batch C reform — Mexico's 2024 judicial elections, Nigeria's
   pending state-police bill, the northern Sharia penal codes — is recorded in prose by dated
   attribution, not as a `ScheduledChange`.
5. **Special-autonomy features stay in prose, not as records** — Indonesia's Aceh sharia, Nigeria's
   Kadhis' and Sharia courts — the same discipline as Finland's Åland counter-example in Batch B.

## Honesty — the load-bearing part of this batch

- **Neutrality on contested reform (tripwire-tested).** Mexico's 2024 judicial-election reform is
  stated as enacted fact with citations, its **assessment** carried only by dated attribution
  (IACHR 2024, OAS 2025); Nigeria's Sharia penal codes (attributed to a 2004 Human Rights Watch
  report, whose human-rights assessments are **not** adopted) and its pending state-police bill
  (attributed to the National Assembly Library Trust Fund, 26 June 2026, **not yet law**) are stated
  by attribution. A mechanical tripwire test scans reader-facing prose and fails on partisan terms.
- **Two-reference-date prison figures, not conflated.** Kenya (population a 2025 average vs a
  December 2023 capacity/occupancy) and Nigeria (population June 2026 vs a June 2025 capacity/
  occupancy) each state the source's dated figures faithfully and **decline** to compute a fresh
  occupancy by mixing the dates; a test asserts the dates are kept distinct.
- **No density where the source gives no current capacity.** New Zealand (2019 capacity) and
  Singapore (2013 capacity) state population and rate only, and make **no** occupancy claim.
- **A low rate is not a claim about conditions.** Nigeria's ≈34 per 100,000 is stated as low **and**
  the same source shows the system above its 2025 capacity — both, without implying the rate speaks
  to conditions.
- **Federations modelled honestly both ways.** India/Mexico/Argentina earned representative
  sub-national records because their states run their own justice functions; South Africa and Kenya
  did **not**, because their provinces/counties run none — the dossiers say so explicitly.
- **WPB figures hand-confirmed at authoring time.** Several moved between research and authoring
  (Indonesia 276,136→287,571; Mexico 265,874→268,245); each figure was re-confirmed by curl and the
  current value used. Every prison claim carries the "not comparable between countries" limitation.
- **Corrections applied from verification** — e.g. NZ's mis-stated Gangs Act aggravator omitted;
  India's dismissed BNSS challenge dropped; South Africa's unattested nickname and active commission
  omitted; Nigeria's NCoS Act dated only to "August 2019"; Kenya's prison-population provenance split
  (national statistics bureau vs UN population base).

## Sources

Load-bearing facts were verified from official primary sources, re-confirmed by hand in an
adversarial pass: the constitutions of **New Zealand** (Constitution Act 1986 + primary statutes),
**South Korea**, **Singapore**, **India** (Seventh Schedule read verbatim), **Indonesia** (1945
Constitution + Aceh law), **Mexico** (2024 judicial-reform decree + IACHR/OAS), **Argentina**,
**South Africa** (1996 Constitution, Schedules 4/5 verbatim), **Kenya** (2010 Constitution, Fourth
Schedule verbatim) and **Nigeria** (1999 Constitution, Second Schedule lists verbatim). Prison
figures come from the **World Prison Brief** (ICPR/Birkbeck), one per-country source, each read
directly and re-confirmed. Contested/pending matters are anchored to named secondary sources by
dated attribution (Korea Herald; IACHR/OAS; Human Rights Watch 2004; the National Assembly Library
Trust Fund). +50 source records (170 → 220).

## Accessibility

The framework's WCAG 2.2 AA guards held for the new pages: single `h1`, skip link and landmarks,
jurisdiction-table caption/region label derived from the country name, and non-Latin / multilingual
names render in the page (Korean, and the Kiswahili and other institutional names). The e2e
accessibility suite (heading structure, landmarks, 320px reflow, 200% zoom, mobile nav) passes for
the whole site.
