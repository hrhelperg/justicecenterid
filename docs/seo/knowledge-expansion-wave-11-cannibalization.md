# Wave 11 — cannibalization analysis

Thirty-six guide candidates, five institution candidates and one profession candidate were
assessed. **Seven routes were published**, all guides, in a new `/defence` section. **No
institution route and no profession route were created.**

Comparison set: the nine `/prosecution/*` guides, the ten `/courts/*` guides, the seven
`/investigations/*` guides, `/law-enforcement/arrest-and-detention`, the glossary,
`/professions/*`, and the 32 country dossiers.

## The constraint that shaped the wave

Unlike Waves 8–10, the constraint here was **source feasibility rather than existing ownership**.
The baseline carried six defence-related sources and no defence content at all. Only three systems
were reached from primary sources — Germany, Brazil and France — and the European Convention on
Human Rights could not be read from an authoritative source at any of five attempted paths.

Every candidate below was therefore tested twice: does it have a distinct intent, and is there
Tier-1 material to write it from. Nine candidates failed the second test having passed the first,
and are deferred rather than rejected.

## Institution candidates

| Candidate                        | Decision   | Reason                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------- | ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Public defender office           | **DEFER**  | Not a recurring family. Germany and France have **no defence institution at all** — a statutory appointment scheme and a funding scheme respectively, both using private practitioners |
| Legal aid authority / commission | **DEFER**  | France administers aid without a body this wave reached; no recurrence established                                                                                                     |
| Court-appointed counsel system   | **REJECT** | A procedure, not an institution                                                                                                                                                        |
| Public defence institution       | **DEFER**  | Brazil has one; two more jurisdictions would be needed                                                                                                                                 |

**Zero institution routes, and the forcing question is why.** The brief asked whether Brazil's
Defensoria Pública belongs to the same family as a United States public defender office. Article
134 makes it a permanent institution essential to the jurisdictional function of the State — the
formula Article 127 uses for the Ministério Público — with a human-rights promotion mandate,
standing in collective and extrajudicial matters, members holding _inamovibilidade_ and forbidden
private practice. A public defender office provides criminal defence to indigent defendants.

They share a function and differ in kind. That is the fourth consecutive wave to find that the
recurring thing is a function whose institutional embodiments differ too much to form a family.

## Boundary compliance

**Prosecution (Wave 10).** No page restates charging, discretion, prosecutorial independence or
accountability. Wave 10's defence-boundary test — which forbids prosecution pages from carrying
public defenders, legal aid, privilege or defence strategy — keeps passing, because this material
went into `/defence`.

**Courts (Wave 9).** No court hierarchy, jurisdiction, appellate structure or trial procedure.

**Investigations (Wave 8).** Linked, never rewritten.

**Criminal procedure.** Trial-stage and appeal-stage defence rights are deliberately deferred
rather than absorbed, so a later criminal-procedure cluster is not pre-empted.

**No redirects.** No existing URL was renamed, removed or re-parented. Seven URLs and one section
index were added.
