# Wave 10 — cannibalization analysis

Thirty-four guide candidates and seven institution candidates were assessed. **Eight routes were
published**, all guides. **No institution route was created.**

Comparison set: `/prosecution/what-does-a-prosecutor-do`, `/professions/prosecutor`, the six
`/investigations/*` guides, the ten `/courts/*` guides, the eight prosecution-related glossary
terms, and the 32 country `prosecution` modules.

## The constraint that shaped the wave

`/professions/prosecutor` owns **who** the prosecutor is. `what-does-a-prosecutor-do` owns **what
the role does**. The glossary owns `prosecutor`, `presumption-of-innocence`, `due-process` and
`disclosure`, and carries unrouted entries for `charging-decision` and `prosecutorial-discretion`.
Wave 8 owns who investigates and who directs whom. Wave 9 owns the court.

What was left unowned is **prosecution as an institutional function** — how the power to charge is
constituted, bounded, insulated and answered for, and how radically that differs between systems.

## Institution candidates

| Candidate | Decision | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public prosecution service | **DEFER** | The *function* recurs everywhere; the *institution* does not. In Nigeria the prosecuting authority is a serving Minister; in the United States there is no national service and most chief local prosecutors are elected; in Germany offices attach to each court rather than forming a service; in Brazil the body sits outside all three branches and its remit is far wider than prosecution. A single institution page would be either empty or wrong about half of them, and the organisational variation is published as a guide instead |
| Prosecutor General office | **REJECT** | The brief's warning is correct on this evidence: Germany's Generalbundesanwalt, Brazil's Procurador-Geral and Spain's Fiscal General occupy radically different constitutional positions. A shared title is not a family |
| Director of Public Prosecutions | **REJECT** | A title, not an institution family. Ireland's and Kenya's offices differ in constitutional footing, and applying the label to France or Germany would misdescribe both |
| Federal prosecution service | **REJECT** | A position within a federal arrangement, covered by the organisation guide |
| State prosecution service | **REJECT** | Same |
| Special prosecutor | **REJECT** | United States terminology; no recurrence established |
| Independent prosecutor | **REJECT** | Not an institution. It is a contested characterisation, which is the subject of a guide |

**Zero institution routes.** This is the same finding Wave 8 reached about judicial police and
Wave 9 reached about supreme courts: where the recurring thing is a function whose institutional
embodiments differ radically, it belongs in a guide, and forcing it into the taxonomy would
manufacture a family the evidence denies.

## The existing guide was corrected, not merely linked

`what-does-a-prosecutor-do` stated that prosecution services "typically apply a two-stage test",
with an evidential stage asking whether there is "a realistic prospect of conviction", on the
authority of two international instruments and **no country-scoped source**.

Germany's § 152(2) StPO obliges the prosecution to act on *sufficient factual indications* and
asks nothing about prospects of conviction. The passage has been rewritten to attribute the
two-stage formulation to the systems that use it, to carry Germany's provision alongside it, and
to cite country-scoped sources for both. This removes an existing universalisation rather than
adding a competing page.

## Boundary compliance

**Defence (Wave 11).** Rights are acknowledged — the accused may answer the case and test the
evidence, and is presumed innocent. No page discusses counsel, public defenders, legal aid,
privilege, equality of arms as a topic, or defence strategy. Asserted by test.

**Investigations (Wave 8).** Linked, never rewritten. No page describes who investigates or who
directs an investigation as its own subject.

**Courts (Wave 9).** No trial procedure, no court hierarchy, no appellate structure. Wave 9's test
forbidding charging and prosecution-independence material on courts pages keeps passing, because
Wave 10's material went to `/prosecution`.

**Professions.** No page describes prosecutorial qualifications, careers or training.

**No redirects.** No existing URL was renamed, removed or re-parented. Eight URLs were added.
